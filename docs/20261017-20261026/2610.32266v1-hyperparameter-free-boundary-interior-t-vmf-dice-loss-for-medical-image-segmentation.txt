ABSTRACT 

Class imbalance and errors near anatomical boundaries re-main challenges in medical image segmentation, motivating loss functions that account for these difficulties. Adaptive t-vMF Dice Loss adjusts the similarity function during training but requires hyperparameter tuning. We propose Self-Tuned Boundary-Aware t-vMF Dice Loss (STB-tvMF Dice), which adapts the function separately for boundary and interior re-gions without tuning shape-adaptation parameters. Each region’s shape parameter of t-vMF function is determined from its mean prediction–target cosine similarity in the pre-ceding epoch and remains fixed throughout the current epoch. This rule maximizes the slope of the similarity function at the observed mean similarity, providing a principled basis for adaptation without validation-based parameter selection. Ex-periments on three datasets and architectures demonstrated its effectiveness. On Synapse with U-Net, STB-tvMF Dice achieved a mean DSC of 70.75%, compared with 63.03% for Adaptive t-vMF Dice Loss. Our code will be available soon. 

Index Terms — Loss function, Semantic segmentation, Medical image, Hyperparameter-free learning, boundary-aware learning 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Medical image segmentation is challenged by substantial class imbalance and ambiguous image information near anatomical boundaries. Loss functions play an important role in determining how prediction errors are evaluated during training. Cross-entropy and Dice losses[1] provide complementary objectives based on pixel-wise classifica-tion and region overlap, respectively. Various extensions have been developed to address their limitations. Tversky loss adjusts the relative contributions of false positives and false negatives, while Focal Tversky loss emphasizes difficult predictions[2, 3]. Other approaches combine cross-entropy and Dice through additive or multiplicative formulations to incorporate both objectives[4]. Beyond selecting which errors to emphasize, the mapping from prediction–target agreement to a loss value determines how improvements in agreement influence optimization. However, its adaptation requires validation data and a manually selected scaling coefficient. Moreover, prediction agreement can differ between bound-ary and interior regions within the same class, motivating adaptation that distinguishes these regions. This raises the question of whether the similarity function can be determined directly from the learning state of each region without manual parameter search. We propose Self-Tuned Boundary-Aware t-vMF Dice Loss (STB-tvMF Dice). The proposed loss partitions pixels into boundary and interior regions using ground-truth labels and evaluates prediction-target cosine similarity separately in each region. For each region, a bounded parameter ρ controls the shape of the similarity function. We derive a self-tuning rule by maximizing the slope of this function at the observed mean cosine similarity. The resulting rule sets ρ to the mean arXiv:2610.32266v1 [cs.CV] 23 Oct 2026 cosine similarity of the corresponding region. Statistics are accumulated during training, and the mean from the preced-ing epoch determines the shape parameter for the current epoch. This procedure adapts the shape parameters using training statistics alone, without validation data or hyperpa-rameter search. Our contributions are twofold. First, we introduce a boundary-interior t-vMF Dice formulation with a self-tuning rule grounded in the local sensitivity of the similarity func-tion, eliminating manual search for loss-specific adaptation parameters. Second, we evaluated the proposed loss through experiments on Kvasir-SEG, ACDC, and Synapse[6, 7, 8] using U-Net, TransUNet, and EMCAD[9, 10, 11]. Compared with the best tested scaling-coefficient settings of Adaptive t-vMF Dice Loss, our method improved the mean Dice simi-larity coefficient (DSC) on Synapse by 7.72 and 2.56% with U-Net and TransUNet, respectively. With EMCAD, our method outperformed all compared losses on Kvasir-SEG and ACDC, and achieved a comparable DSC on Synapse for the best Adaptive t-vMF setting. Our method achieved these results without manual search for shape-adaptation parame-ters. 

2. METHODOLOGY 

We propose Self-Tuned Boundary-Aware t-vMF Dice Loss (STB-tvMF Dice). Unlike Adaptive t-vMF Dice Loss[5], which uses class-wise validation performance and a manually selected scaling coefficient, our method determines separate boundary and interior shape parameters directly from training similarities. 

2.1. Boundary-Interior Similarity 

Let pn,k (u) denote the predicted probability of class k at pixel 

u in image n, and let yn,k (u) be the corresponding one-hot ground-truth label. We identify boundary pixels where the ground-truth label differs from its local average computed by 

3×3 average pooling with stride one and zero padding; the re-maining pixels form the interior region. The resulting binary masks are denoted by mn,b and mn,i and are shared across classes. The neighborhood size is fixed across all datasets, and any ignored pixels are excluded from both masks. For each region r ∈ { b, i }, we compute cosine similarity between the masked prediction and target: 

xn,k,r = ⟨N (pn,k ⊙ mn,r ), N (yn,k ⊙ mn,r )⟩ , (1) where ⊙ denotes element-wise multiplication and N (v) = 

v/ max( ∥v∥2, ϵ norm ). The norm and inner product are com-puted over spatial positions. Boundary and interior agreement are thus evaluated separately, with one shape parameter per region shared across classes.

3. EXPERIMENTS 3.1. Datasets and Metrics 

We evaluate the proposed method on three medical im-age segmentation datasets: Kvasir-SEG for polyp segmen-tation in endoscopic images[6], ACDC for cardiac MRI segmentation[7], and Synapse for abdominal multi-organ CT segmentation[8]. The datasets contain 2, 4, and 9 classes, including background, respectively. We conduct cross-validation using identical fold assign-ments across the compared losses. Segmentation perfor-mance is measured by the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC). We report the mean DSC across all classes, including back-ground, averaged over all folds. Evaluation is performed on 2D images or slices.

3.3. Quantitative comparison 

Table 1 summarizes segmentation performance across three datasets and three architectures. STB-tvMF Dice achieved the highest mean DSC in six of the nine settings and remained close to the best-performing baseline in the remaining three. On Synapse, it improved DSC over Dice loss by 5.73 and 3.56% with U-Net and TransUNet, respectively. Compared with the best tested λ of Adaptive t-vMF Dice Loss, the corresponding gains are 7.72 and 2.56%. With EMCAD, it achieved the highest DSC on Kvasir-SEG and ACDC. These results demonstrate competitive performance across archi-tectures using training statistics alone for shape adaptation, without validation-based shape updates or manual search for adaptation parameters.

3.5. Ablation studies 

Table 2 uses the same self-tuning rule and epoch-wise up-dates, but changes how ρ is assigned: one shared across all classes and pixels, one per class, or one each for boundary and interior regions. The boundary-interior formulation achieved the highest DSC on all three datasets. On Synapse, it out-performed the global and class-wise variants by 18.34 and 23.37%, respectively. Pooling statistics across classes within each region may provide more reliable estimates than class-wise adaptation for infrequently occurring classes. Table 3 fixed the boundary-interior formulation and com-pared four parameter-update strategies using U-Net: batch-wise updates, EMA with momentum coefficients of 0.9 and 0.99, and epoch-wise averaging. The DSC values differed by at most 0.84% on each dataset. Epoch-wise averag-ing achieved the highest DSC on Kvasir-SEG and Synapse, whereas batch-wise updates performed best on ACDC. We adopted epoch-wise averaging because it maintained compet-itive accuracy across datasets without introducing an EMA momentum coefficient. Table 4 examines the boundary extraction kernel size us-ing U-Net. DSC varies by less than 0.5 % on Kvasir-SEG and ACDC, while increasing the kernel size from 3 × 3 to 7 × 7

reduces DSC by 2.06 % on Synapse. These results support using a fixed 3 × 3 neighborhood across datasets. 

4. CONCLUSION 

We proposed STB-tvMF Dice, a loss function that separately adapts boundary and interior similarity functions using train-ing statistics alone. Maximizing the local sensitivity of the function yields a simple update rule that sets each shape parameter to the preceding epoch’s mean regional cosine similarity, eliminating manual search for adaptation param-eters. Experiments on three datasets and three architectures demonstrated competitive segmentation performance, while ablation studies supported the boundary-interior formulation and epoch-wise updates.
