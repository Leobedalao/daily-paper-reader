ABSTRACT 

Medical image segmentation remains challenging when tar-gets exhibit limited visual distinction, irregular structure, or substantial variation across imaging modalities. Medi-cal text can reduce this ambiguity, but indiscriminate fu-sion may introduce unreliable guidance. We present RELiF, a vision–language framework that integrates text through reliability-guided decoding. Its Reliability-guided Fusion Gate (RFG) adaptively controls cross-modal updates, while the Text-guided Skip Refinement Adapter (TSRA) refines high-resolution features using text guidance. RELiF is eval-uated on radiographic, CT, and endoscopic segmentation tasks, where it shows competitive performance across diverse imaging characteristics and target appearances. Additional analyses examine efficiency and stability. The complemen-tary uncertainty representation derived from the reliability response also shows an association with prediction diffi-culty, with higher uncertainty values occurring more fre-quently in pixels associated with segmentation errors. 1. INTRODUCTION 

Medical image segmentation supports clinical assessment, treatment planning, and disease monitoring by identifying anatomical structures and pathological regions. Unimodal approaches, such as U-Net [1], rely solely on visual informa-tion, which can limit their effectiveness when targets exhibit subtle appearances or substantial variation across imaging conditions. To address these challenges, multimodal ap-proaches incorporate complementary semantic information from medical text, building on advances in vision–language learning such as CLIP [2]. However, the benefits of textual guidance depend not only on the information it provides but also on how effectively it is integrated with visual features.

Recent studies have explored cross-modal reconstruc-tion [3], uncertainty-aware learning [4], and bidirectional fu-sion [5] to improve vision–language segmentation. However, how to adaptively regulate textual guidance across different visual regions during decoding remains an open question, as its relevance may vary spatially. Another limitation lies in conventional skip connections, which directly transfer high-resolution features without considering their relevance, potentially introducing domain-specific variations into the decoder [6]. These challenges call for selective cross-modal fusion and context-aware refinement of skip features. To address these challenges, we propose RELiF, a vision– language framework that combines reliability-guided decod-ing with text-guided skip refinement. Its Reliability-guided Fusion Gate (RFG) estimates token-wise reliability from vi-sual and text-conditioned features, together with their feature-level agreement and discrepancy, to adaptively regulate cross-modal contributions during decoding. The main contributions are as follows: • We introduce RELiF, whose Reliability-guided Fusion Gate (RFG) within the Reliability-guided Decoding At-tention Block (RDAB) adaptively regulates cross-modal fusion using token-wise reliability derived from visual and text-conditioned features and their feature-level simi-larities and differences. • We develop the Text-guided Skip Refinement Adapter (TSRA) to preserve fine structural details through text-conditioned channel modulation and lightweight refine-ment of high-resolution skip features. • We evaluate RELiF on chest X-ray and lung CT infec-tion segmentation, as well as endoscopic polyp segmenta-tion, demonstrating its effectiveness across diverse imag-ing modalities and target appearances. • We conduct additional experiments and analyses to assess RELiF’s effectiveness, reliability, efficiency, and stability. 

2. METHODOLOGY 2.1. Overview 

As shown in Fig. 2, visual and textual features guide progres-sive decoding. RDABs regulate cross-modal fusion, while TSRAs operate on separate skip pathways, refining encoder features before their integration into decoder blocks. Finally, subpixel upsampling restores the spatial resolution, and the segmentation head generates the final pixel-wise predictions.

2.5. Objective Function 

RELiF is trained using the Spectral-Entropic Uncertainty (SEU) objective [4], which combines three complementary loss terms. Dice loss maximizes overlap between the pre-dicted mask cM and ground truth M, while spectral loss encourages structural consistency by comparing their Fourier magnitude spectra. An entropy-inspired term further pro-motes confident foreground predictions. The overall objective is defined as 

Ltotal = LDice + λF LSpectral + λE LEntropy , (8) where λF and λE control the contributions of the spectral and entropy terms, respectively. The entropy-inspired term is applied only to the final segmentation probabilities and is sep-arate from the RFG-derived uncertainty representation. RFG and TSRA are learned end-to-end without direct supervision. 

3. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS 3.1. Datasets 

Experiments were conducted on three public datasets: QaTa-COV19 [9], MosMedData+ [10], and Kvasir-SEG [11], cov-ering chest X-ray, CT, and endoscopic segmentation. Follow-ing the LViT protocol [12], we adopt the same dataset splits and use text annotations from [12] for infection segmentation and [13] for polyp segmentation. The dataset splits are: QaTa-COV19: Training=5716; Validation=1429; Test=2113 MosMedData+: Training=2183; Validation=273; Test=273 Kvasir-SEG: Training=800; Validation=100; Test=100. 

3.2. Implementation Details 

The framework was implemented in PyTorch, with each dataset trained independently on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. All input images and masks were resized to 224 × 224 pixels. Training was conducted for 200 epochs, using batch sizes of 32 for training and 8 for validation and testing. We used the AdamW optimizer with a cosine annealing learning-rate scheduler. Initial learning rates were 3 × 10 −4 for QaTa-COV19 and Kvasir-SEG and 5 × 10 −4 for MosMedData+. The SEU loss weights were set to λF = 0 .3 and λE = 0 .1.

4. CONCLUSION 

This paper introduced RELiF, combining reliability-guided fusion (RFG) and text-conditioned skip refinement (TSRA) for medical segmentation. The results suggest benefits from complementary mechanisms under limited supervision. Sen-sitivity to mismatched text indicates that gains depend on rele-vant guidance rather than text alone. RFG-derived uncertainty helps identify unreliable regions, although excessive rejection may remove correctly segmented foreground. These findings motivate further reliability-aware segmentation research.
