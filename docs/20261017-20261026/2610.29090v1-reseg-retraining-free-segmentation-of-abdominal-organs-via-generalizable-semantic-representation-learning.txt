Abstract
Abdominal organ segmentation is essential in modern medical applications
including computer-aided diagnosis, therapy planning, and surgical naviga-
tion. While existing deep learning approaches demonstrate promising per-
formance, their heavy reliance on large-scale patient image data covering
multiple domains raises concerns over generalization capability and privacy
risks. This motivates the development of generalizable segmentation train-
ing paradigms based on domain-randomized image synthesis, where networks
are trained using synthesized images with diverse contrasts and resolutions,
facilitating generalization across unseen real image domains. However, ex-
isting paradigms either suffer from limited segmentation performance when
applied to abdominal organs, or require dataset-specific finetuning. To ad-
dress these limitations, we propose ReSeg, a novel two-stage retraining-free
framework for domain-agnostic zero-shot abdominal organ segmentation. At
the first stage, our organ-agnostic synthetic data-driven pretraining approach
exploits semantically and visually diverse synthetic images generated by a
dedicated data engine to optimize a Generalizable Semantic Representation
Network (GSRNet). The network is thereby capable of learning semantic
features robust against variations in anatomical structure, imaging contrast,
resolution and spatial context, which provide universal representation capa-
bilities that serve as a foundation for segmentation learning. Subsequently,
in the second stage, we continue to train an Organ-specific Encoder-decoder
Segmentation Network (OESNet), whose encoder is initialized with the pre-
trained weights. This training process is facilitated by another dedicated
data engine, which generates diversified visual images while strictly retain-
ing the inherent spatial layout of abdominal organs.

1. Introduction
Abdominal organ segmentation plays a pivotal role in medical imaging
by enabling precise organ localization and volumetric analysis, serving as a
foundation for computer-aided diagnosis (Chan et al., 2020), therapy plan-
ning (Chen et al., 2021), and surgical navigation (Okamoto et al., 2015).
For instance, liver segmentation aids focal lesion detection and liver fibrosis
staging (Tong et al., 2015; Gao et al., 2023), and kidney segmentation from
preoperative scans is a prerequisite for building 3D models that support aug-
mented reality navigation in laparoscopic partial nephrectomy (Zhang et al.,
2019). Manual segmentation of abdominal organs is time-consuming, labor-
intensive, and error-prone, thus spurring the need for accurate automatic
segmentation algorithms.
Automated segmentation of abdominal organs from volumetric medical
images is a challenging task. First, soft tissues usually have low contrast
characteristic in abdominal imaging, leading to weak boundaries between
adjacent anatomical structures (Wang et al., 2019). Second, the abdominal
cavity presents a complex background with highly variable morphological
structures, whose shapes and sizes exhibit significant heterogeneity among
patients (Ma et al., 2021). Third, abdominal images acquired from differ-
ent clinical settings often vary substantially in terms of field of view (FOV),
introducing inconsistencies in the contextual information associated with or-
gans.
The field has witnessed remarkable progress through deep learning ap-
proaches to meet the challenges mentioned above (Isensee et al., 2021; Liu
et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025). This progress has shifted
the research focus from dataset-specific models towards universal founda-
tion models, which offer improved performance while enhancing generaliza-
tion capability. Some studies leverage approaches inspired by Contrastive
Language-Image Pretraining (CLIP) (Liu et al., 2023) or Segment Anything
Model (SAM) (Du et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024; He et al., 2025) to handle di-
verse segmentation tasks, while others advance representation learning meth-
ods to pretrain networks with powerful feature extraction capability. These
include reconstruction-based methods like MAE (He et al., 2022), SimMIM
(Xie et al., 2022), and MiM (Zhuang et al., 2025a), as well as contrastive
learning approaches such as VoCo (Wu et al., 2024), UAE (Bai et al., 2025),
and SuperCL (Zeng et al., 2026). Finetuning these pretrained networks on
downstream segmentation datasets has yielded promising results.
3


Despite these advantages, most existing biomedical foundation segmen-
tation methods face a fundamental limitation: they require large amounts
of medical image datasets covering multiple domains to achieve excellent
generalization performance and inherently have high reliance on real med-
ical images for training (Liu et al., 2023; Du et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024;
He et al., 2025). Such reliance introduces not only privacy risks but also
modality-specific biases, as significant domain gaps persist with large-scale
data (Huang et al., 2025), leading models to learn suboptimal feature rep-
resentations. This results in limited generalization in real clinical scenarios,
particularly when processing images from entirely unseen biomedical scenar-
ios with variant imaging modalities, such as Computed Tomography (CT),
Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), and Positron Emission Tomography
(PET). Hence, there is an urgent need for a domain-agnostic zero-shot seg-
mentation approach, where the model can segment images from unseen do-
mains with cross-modality discrepancies and intra-modality variations.
Recently, there have been research efforts dedicated to developing general-
izable segmentation training paradigms based on domain-randomized image
synthesis strategies in brain MR image analysis (Billot et al., 2023; Ayaz
et al., 2024; Gopinath et al., 2025). These approaches train Convolutional
Neural Networks (CNNs) exclusively on synthetic images sampled from a
generative model conditioned on label maps, eliminating the requirement
for real training images. By leveraging a domain randomization strategy,
they generate pseudo training images with varying contrasts and resolutions,
exposing models to extensive variability during training.
This forces the
models to learn domain-independent features, enabling effective generaliza-
tion to real clinical scans across diverse domains during inference. Beyond
brain imaging, such a paradigm has also been extended in existing literature
to segmentation tasks targeting other anatomical structures, including vas-
cular structures (Chollet et al., 2024) and cellular nuclei (Dey et al., 2024).
Building on the domain randomization concept, Anatomix (Dey et al., 2025)
further introduced a general-purpose representation learning framework pow-
ered by a dedicated data engine, which synthesizes highly variable training
samples from compositions of organ shape templates. This enables the pre-
training of a network that serves as a strong, dataset-agnostic initialization
for finetuning on new segmentation datasets.
Despite significant progress achieved so far, to achieve generalizable seg-
mentation of abdominal organs without access to real training images, several
challenges persist. First, most existing approaches are designed for struc-

tures with relatively fixed anatomical topology and stable tissue intensity
distribution such as the brain (Billot et al., 2023), the vascular structures
(Chollet et al., 2024), and the cellular nuclei (Dey et al., 2024), and thus
fall short in segmentation performance when applied to abdominal organs
(as demonstrated by the experimental results presented in Fig. 1), which ex-
hibit large morphological variations among patients. Second, it is challenging
to generate realistic synthetic abdominal data due to the lack of standard-
ized anatomical atlases and refined annotated templates, resulting in severe
domain gaps between synthetic and real clinical scans.
Third, the exist-
ing domain-randomization-based representation learning method Anatomix
(Dey et al., 2025) focuses on acquiring appearance-agnostic representations,
yet overlooks the contextual consistency in the learned features. This may
lead to diminished performance when segmenting images across varying FOVs
where contextual information changes. Finally, although the existing repre-
sentation learning method Anatomix (Dey et al., 2025) enhances domain-
agnostic features, additional finetuning on downstream datasets is required
for adapting the pretrained network to task-specific segmentation, limiting
their applicability in retraining-free settings.
To address these limitations, we first propose a Generalizable Appearance
(GA) model to generate diversified pseudo-images based on organ shape tem-
plates, thereby eliminating the reliance on real annotated images for network
training. This model realizes full randomization of image contrast and res-
olution via domain randomization. On this basis, we further develop two
dedicated data engines, namely GenRep and GenSeg, which are respectively
devised for organ-agnostic semantic representation learning and abdominal-
organ-specific segmentation learning. Specifically, GenRep generates image
samples with rich semantic layouts and visual variations to capture organ-
agnostic, generalizable semantic representations. In contrast, GenSeg yields
appearance-variant pseudo-images while retaining inherent anatomical spa-
tial configurations. Combined with background simulation empowered by
a newly designed Label Processing (LP) model, it enables optimization for
abdominal organ segmentation without any real training images.
Built upon these two data engines, we propose a two-stage retraining-free
framework, referred to as ReSeg, for real-training-image-free abdominal or-
gan segmentation, as visualized in Fig. 1-(a). Specifically, at the first stage,
our organ-agnostic synthetic data-driven pretraining approach exploits se-
mantically and visually diverse synthetic images generated by the GenRep
data engine to optimize a Generalizable Semantic Representation Network

(GSRNet).
The network is thereby capable of learning semantic features
robust against variations in anatomical structure, imaging contrast, resolu-
tion and spatial context, which provide universal representation capabilities
that serve as a foundation for segmentation learning. This is done by de-
signing a novel Multi-patch Context-aware Contrastive (MCC) loss that en-
courages domain-invariant feature alignment amidst contextual variations.
Subsequently, in the second stage, we continue to train an Organ-specific
Encoder-decoder Segmentation Network (OESNet), whose encoder is initial-
ized with the pretrained weights. This training process is facilitated by the
GenSeg data engine, which generates diversified visual images while strictly
retaining the inherent spatial layout of abdominal organs. To enhance organ-
relevant region perception, OESNet further incorporates a Semantics-guided
Segmentation (SS) decoder, which hierarchically integrates rich semantic pri-
ors derived from the well-trained GSRNet.
During inference, ReSeg can
segment real clinical images from multiple domains without retraining or
finetuning.
ReSeg achieves superior performance compared with existing
state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods on seven abdominal segmentation datasets

and abdominal-organ-specific segmentation learning.
• Built upon these two data engines, we propose ReSeg, a novel two-stage

2. Related work
2.1. Dataset-specific models for medical image segmentation
The unprecedented developments of deep learning have revolutionized the
field of medical image segmentation, driving a paradigm shift from traditional
methods (Wolz et al., 2013; Tong et al., 2015) toward deep learning-based
approaches (Isensee et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2022). The U-shaped encoder-
decoder framework, exemplified by U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) with
skip connections and multi-scale feature extraction, has become the dominant
architecture. Numerous variants have been developed to further improve seg-
mentation accuracy for various anatomical structures, like abdominal organs
(Ma et al., 2021; Ji et al., 2022). One strategy integrates attention mecha-
nisms to recalibrate feature responses and emphasize target regions, thereby
mitigating interference from complex abdominal background and enhancing
boundary delineation of ambiguous organs (Wang et al., 2019; Zheng et al.,
2025). Transformer-based architectures have also been adopted to capture
long-range spatial dependencies across volumetric abdominal images, com-
plementing local CNN features with a global perspective (Cao et al., 2022;
Ding et al., 2026). More recently, Mamba-based models have emerged as
promising alternatives, offering long-range modeling with linear complexity
and thus balancing performance and computational efficiency for volumetric
abdominal image segmentation (Ruan et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Tan et al.,
2026). Despite these advances, these methods are typically trained on a sin-
gle dataset and thus remain dataset-specific, lacking the ability to generalize
across heterogeneous imaging domains.
2.2. Self-supervised learning for medical image segmentation
To improve model generalization capability, remarkable progress has been
witnessed in developing foundation models pretrained on large-scale medical
image datasets (Butoi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Du et al., 2024; Wu
et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024; He et al., 2025). A key direction within this ef-
fort involves designing self-supervised proxy tasks to enhance representation
7


learning. These approaches typically fall into two categories. The first em-
ploys reconstructive losses to model anatomical structures and their spatial
relationships, with representative works including MAE (He et al., 2022),
SimMIM (Xie et al., 2022), and MiM (Zhuang et al., 2025a). The second
category, by contrast, utilizes contrastive losses to learn consistent seman-
tic representations. This is achieved by enforcing feature similarity within a
predefined semantic space, as exemplified by VoCo (Wu et al., 2024), UAE
(Bai et al., 2025), and SuperCL (Zeng et al., 2026).
Despite significant
progress, existing representation learning methods remain limited by their
high reliance on heuristics tailored to their target datasets and typically
learn feature similarity within a single imaging modality. This leads to com-
promised generalization when applied to new biomedical domains. Although
UAE (Bai et al., 2025) extends feature learning to cross-modality CT-MRI
scenarios, its iterative training process is computationally intensive, and the
learned features remain insufficient under significant domain shifts (e.g. CT
or MRI to PET). To gain domain-agnostic performance, our work introduces
domain-randomization-based data engines that synthesize images with di-
verse resolutions and contrasts, thereby facilitating generalizable semantic
representation learning.
2.3. Domain randomization for medical image segmentation
Domain-randomization generative models seeks to bridge all domain gaps
through extreme augmentation beyond realism without acquiring real sam-
ples (Tobin et al., 2017; Tremblay et al., 2018). These methods fully random-
ize the generation parameters to produce highly unrealistic samples, in which
case real images are encompassed within the synthetic data spectrum (Trem-
blay et al., 2018). SynthSeg (Billot et al., 2023) pioneered this paradigm
for brain segmentation by training a 3D UNet (Ronneberger et al., 2015)
exclusively on vastly different synthetic images sampled from a generative
model conditioned on brain label maps. This facilitates the network to learn
modality-agnostic features, enabling segmentation of real brain MR scans
from a wide range of target domains without requiring retraining.
Sub-
sequent researches have adopted this domain-randomization-based training
framework for brain scan segmentation (Ayaz et al., 2024; Gopinath et al.,
2025), while other studies have extended its application to vascular structures
(Chollet et al., 2024) and cellular nuclei (Dey et al., 2024).
Although these methods enable generalizable segmentation without re-
training, their direct application to abdominal organ segmentation remains
8


limited in performance. This is primarily due to several intrinsic challenges
posed by abdominal anatomy.
Unlike the structures targeted by existing
approaches such as the brain (Billot et al., 2023), the vascular structures
(Chollet et al., 2024), and the cellular nuclei (Dey et al., 2024), which exhibit
relatively fixed anatomical topology and stable tissue intensity distribution,
abdominal organs reside in a complex background and show large morpho-
logical variations among patients. This problem is further compounded by
the lack of standardized anatomical atlases and refined annotated templates
for the abdomen, making it difficult to generate realistic synthetic abdominal
data. In contrast, our method is specifically designed for real-training-image-
free abdominal organ segmentation by incorporating a generalizable appear-
ance model and a label processing model into the pseudo-image synthesis
pipeline, thereby enabling the network to effectively learn abdominal-organ-
specific semantic features.
To learn general-purpose features, Anatomix (Dey et al., 2025) proposed
a representation learning framework driven by a dedicated data engine that
randomizes spatial configurations of organ shape templates to synthesize
paired image samples with distinct appearances. During pretraining, a con-
trastive learning strategy is employed to encourage a network to be stable
against nuisance imaging variations that do not change image semantics.
The pretrained network can then be used to extract robust, domain-agnostic
representations for downstream tasks.
Nevertheless, this method primar-
ily focuses on appearance invariance while neglecting contextual consistency,
which is crucial for segmenting images with contextual changes across vary-
ing FOVs. Additionally, finetuning on downstream datasets is required for
image segmentation, which may introduce complexity for clinical users. To
bridge these gaps, we propose a two-stage retraining-free framework that first
learns organ-agnostic semantic features robust to appearance and contextual
changes, and then transitions to learning organ-specific semantic features
with the guidance of these semantic priors.

a Semantics-guided Segmentation (SS) decoder. This decoder enhances seg-

ors derived from the well-trained GSRNet.
During inference, OESNet achieves broad generalization across multiple
image domains, effectively handling diverse modalities with varying acqui-
sition conditions including vendors, scanning protocols, imaging sequences,
and patient populations. Organ segmentations for real images are achieved
without requiring domain-specific adaptation (e.g., retraining or finetuning).

3.2. Data engines
Our pipeline only leverages organ shape templates to synthesize diverse
pseudo training images. To this end, we propose a Generalizable Appear-
ance (GA) model that generates pseudo-images conditioned on label maps
derived from the available templates. This model employs domain random-
ization to fully randomize image contrasts and resolutions. Different from
existing methods (Billot et al., 2023; Dey et al., 2025), we introduce a dy-
namic lower bound strategy in the Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) for
intensity sampling. This strategy substantially enhances variation in image
contrast (see Fig. 3) and boosts the asymptotic scaling of contrast variance
(see Supplement A for detailed mathematical proof), thereby increasing the
diversity of synthetic images to improve model generalization capability.
Built upon the GA model, we designed two dedicated data engines based
on different parameter sets and distinct label maps to respectively construct
two synthetic databases, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The first engine, referred to
as GenRep, generates image samples with diverse semantic layouts and ap-
pearances for organ-agnostic semantic representation learning. It first creates
randomized label maps from randomly sampled shape templates across all
patients. This randomization strategy increases semantic layout diversity,
thereby enabling effective representation learning. Each randomized label
map is then used to synthesize paired images with distinct appearances us-
ing the GA model, simulating cross-domain data. These randomized label
maps with their paired synthetic images form the RPI database.
The second engine, namely GenSeg, is designed for abdominal-organ-
specific segmentation learning. It produces image samples with varying vi-
sual appearances while preserving the inherent spatial layout of abdominal
organs. Anatomical label maps are derived from the segmentation masks of
all organs from the same patients, while maintaining the spatial arrangements
of anatomical structures. Additionally, background simulation is integrated
through a Label Processing (LP) model to support effective feature learn-
ing for abdominal organ segmentation. We then sample intensities based
on these anatomical label maps using the GA model, resulting in the AMI
database.

3.2.2. Data engine for representation learning
GenRep is designed for organ-agnostic semantic representation learning.

of semantic layouts and appearances, as illustrated in Fig. 4-(a). Following

from the segmentation masks across all patients. Subsequently, a label as-
sembling model applies random spatial deformations to the shape templates
and then composite them together. The assembled label map is cropped or
padded to a fixed size of 128 × 128 × 128 voxels.

GenSeg is designed to generate diverse pseudo-images that retain inherent
anatomical spatial configurations for abdominal-organ-specific segmentation
learning. For a randomly drawn patient, segmentations of all organs {Sorgan}
are assembled to get a patient-specific label map while preserving the inherent
spatial layout of abdominal organs (see Fig. 4-(b) for an example). Then,
we generate pseudo-data through two steps: 1) image synthesis for training
input and 2) label selection for training supervision.

organs is constructed by a successive morphological closing procedure (see
Fig. 4-(b) for an illustration). This process involves (B + 1) iterative closing

Context-aware Semantic (CS) decoder and an Appearance-aware Semantic

4. Experiments and results
This section first describes the experimental setup, then reports segmen-
tation performance across multiple target domains including CT, MRI and
PET modalities, and proceeds to validate the effectiveness of the proposed
method through comprehensive ablation studies. Finally, the generalizability
of our method to different anatomical structures is showcased via extension
to the cardiac segmentation task.
4.1. Datasets
To assess segmentation generalization capability, we evaluated on seven
publicly available abdominal datasets across CT, MRI and PET modalities.
Among them, four datasets (two CT datasets and two MR datasets), referred
to as the core evaluation datasets, were used for comprehensive evaluation,
including comparisons among zero-shot methods, among few-shot methods,
and against reference baselines (lower bound and upper bound), as well as
ablation analysis. The remaining three datasets (one CT dataset, one MR
dataset and one PET dataset), referred to as the generalization evaluation
datasets, were used solely to assess zero-shot generalization performance.
The four core evaluation datasets are as follows:
• BTCV: From the Multi-Atlas Labeling Beyond The Cranial Vault
workshop held at MICCAI 2015 (Landman et al., 2015), this dataset
contains 30 abdominal CT volumes with 13 annotated organs.
For
training comparison models that require images in the same domain
(dataset-specific methods), we randomly split the data into 15 train-
ing/validation and 15 held-out test cases.
• L2RAb-MR: From the Learn2Reg Abdomen MRI-CT challenge (Her-
ing et al., 2022), this collection combines 40 T2-SPIR MR images from
the Combined Healthy Abdominal Organ Segmentation (CHAOS) chal-
lenge (Kavur et al., 2021) and 8 multi-phase MR images from The
Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA) project (Clark et al., 2013), each of
which has 4 annotated organs. For quality control, we excluded 3 cases
with erroneous annotations in automatically segmented CHAOS test
subset provided by Learn2Reg, leaving 45 valid volumes. These were
randomly divided into 23 training/validation cases for dataset-specific
methods and 22 held-out test samples.
27


• AMOS-CT: This dataset is from the Multi-Modality Abdominal Multi-
Organ Segmentation Challenge (Ji et al., 2022) held at MICCAI 2022,
which provides 500 CT scans collected from multi-center, multi-vendor,
and multi-disease patients, each with 15 annotated organs. The data
were officially partitioned into 200 training, 100 validation, and 200
private test cases. We adopted the original training set (200) as the
training and validation data for dataset-specific methods and the offi-
cial validation set (100) as the test data.
• AMOS-MR: The MR counterpart of AMOS-CT (Ji et al., 2022) con-
tains 100 multi-phase scans (15 annotated organs) with official splits:
40 training, 20 validation, and 40 private test volumes. We utilized the
40 training scans for training and validation of dataset-specific methods
and the original 20 validation cases for testing.
The three generalization evaluation datasets are as follows:
• WORD: The Whole abdominal ORgan Dataset (Luo et al., 2022)
consists of 150 CT volumes, each with 16 annotated organs. All the
cases were used for validating the generalization capability of models.
• MRISegmentator: An abdominal MR dataset (Zhuang et al., 2024,
2025b) comprises of axial precontrast T1, arterial, venous, and delayed
phases for each patient. Each scan contains 62 annotated abdominal
structures. We used the official 240 test cases to evaluate the general-
ization capability of models.
• AutoPET-Organ: This abdominal PET dataset is extended from
the AutoPET dataset (Gatidis et al., 2022) by Zhang et al. (2025),
who annotated 12 organs on a small subset with 100 cases from the
original 1,014 3D whole-body PET images. All the annotated cases
were employed for testing.
For all datasets, to generate the GT segmentation labels for evaluation,
we extracted the labels corresponding to our target abdominal organs (the
liver, the spleen, the right kidney, and the left kidney) from the annotations,
renumbered them consecutively, and assigned the background label to non-
target structures.

4.2. Comparison methods
We compared ReSeg against both zero-shot and few-shot approaches:
• SAM-based zero-shot methods: These methods leverage founda-
tion models like SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) for data-efficient 2D med-
ical image segmentation.
We benchmarked two SOTA SAM-based
methods: 1) MedCLIP-SAM (Koleilat et al., 2024), which imple-
ments text-prompted segmentation in a strict zero-shot setting; and
2) ZeroShotSAM (Shaharabany and Wolf, 2024), which performs
minimally-guided zero-shot segmentation using M foreground points
per 2D slice as prompts.
• Synthetic data-based zero-shot method: We compared against
SynthSeg (Billot et al., 2023), a method that demonstrates domain
generalization for brain scans by training exclusively on synthetic data
generated via a domain randomization strategy.
In our abdominal
organ segmentation experiment, we also employed the segmentation
masks in TotalSegmentator CT dataset (Wasserthal et al., 2023) to
train SynthSeg (Billot et al., 2023) for fair comparison.
• Meta-learning-based few-shot methods: These methods segment
unseen classes by capturing similarities between K labeled support
samples and test images. We compared three SOTA prototype-based
few-shot approaches: SSL-ALPNet (Ouyang et al., 2022), DSPNet
(Tang et al., 2025), and DIFD (Cheng et al., 2025). All models are
trained on in-domain real images using self-supervised learning with
superpixel pseudo-labels. For training, we followed the less strict ex-
perimental setting in Roy et al. (2020), where test classes may ap-
pear in training data but remain unlabeled. In addition, we compared
UniverSeg (Butoi et al., 2023), a typical few-shot foundation model
trained on a large scale of medical image datasets.
• Finetuning-based few-shot methods: These methods implement
few-shot segmentation by first pretraining a strong biomedical founda-
tion model and then finetune it with K annotated volumes. To achieve
strong performance, extensive augmentation and full-network finetun-
ing are employed.
We compared two SOTA representation learning
methods: 1) VoCo (Wu et al., 2024), a simple-yet-effective volume con-
trast framework leveraging geometric relations between different organs
29


for pretraining, which requires real training images; and 2) Anatomix
(Dey et al., 2025), a representation learning method using a domain
randomization strategy to synthesize highly variable training samples,
enabling networks to acquire general-purpose biomedical volume rep-
resentations during pretraining.
To quantify the performance of different competing methods, we trained
two additional models as the reference baselines for each core evaluation
dataset.
These two models were developed using nnUNet (Isensee et al.,
2021), a SOTA supervised method that automatically configures preprocess-
ing, network architecture, and training. The first model, referred to as the
upper bound model, was trained on the training data of one dataset and
then tested on the testing data of the same dataset. In contrast, the sec-
ond model, referred to as the lower bound model, was trained and tested
on datasets from different modalities. Specifically, the lower bound model
for the CT datasets was trained on the L2RAb-MR dataset, while the lower
bound model for the MR datasets was trained on the BTCV CT dataset.
4.3. Implementation details
The proposed ReSeg framework was implemented using PyTorch v2.1.
For the pretraining stage, we configured each decoder with an embedding
dimension of 128. During contrastive learning, the AS decoder processed
entire paired synthetic volumes of size 128 × 128 × 128 with batch size 1,
while the CS decoder utilized six overlapping patches of size 96 × 96 × 96
with batch size 2 (S0 = 96). We empirically set rL = 0.8, rH = 1.2, Nanc =
256, Ncan = 512, τc = 0.33 and τa = 0.1.
We used Stochastic Gradient
Descent (SGD) with a momentum of 0.9 and a weight decay of 1.0 × 10−4,
employing a learning rate of 0.02 with warmup and step decay scheduling.
This pretraining phase lasted for a total of 45,000 iterations.
For the segmentation learning stage, we used input patches of size 128 ×
128 × 128 with 3.0 × 3.0 × 3.0 mm3 spacing. Training employed a batch
size of 2, and OESNet was optimized using SGD with the same momentum
and weight decay parameters as in the pretraining stage, but with a reduced
learning rate of 0.01 that similarly incorporated warmup and step decay.
This stage required 80,000 training iterations.

4.4. Evaluation protocol
To evaluate the capability of the segmentation models, we employed the
Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) (Dice, 1945), which is a standard metric
used to evaluate 3D medical image segmentation (Isensee et al., 2021). DSC
quantifies the spatial overlap between segmentation predictions and GT seg-
mentation labels. Following the evaluation protocol introduced in (Isensee
et al., 2021), we first computed the DSC score for each target organ per case,
then averaged the scores across all cases to get the organ-wise DSC scores,
and finally derived the average DSC by averaging across all target organs.
The DSC scores were evaluated in the original space of the testing 3D
volumes. For 2D benchmarks, we processed volumetric data through vertical
axial slice decomposition. For 3D models, we directly processed the entire
3D images to predict 3D segmentation masks.

4.6.2. Results of comparing the MCC loss against its variants
In the pretraining stage, GSRNet is optimized by a novel MCC loss that
encourages domain-invariant feature alignment amidst contextual variations
through a CS decoder. To verify the effectiveness of the context-aware con-
trastive strategy, we compared the proposed MCC loss against two ablation
variants that did not consider contextual consistency during pretraining:
• AS decoder-only variant: The GSRNet only had the AS decoder
trained by the DPC loss.
• Dual-decoder variant: The GSRNet had the AS decoder trained by
the DPC loss, and another decoder trained by a voxel-wise contrastive
loss using the original image pairs, which is the loss used in Anatomix
(Dey et al., 2025).
Results of this ablation study are summarized in Table 5. From this table,
one can see that our multi-patch context-aware contrastive strategy outper-
formed both ablation variants, achieving an average DSC of 88.45% across
the four abdominal datasets. This represents an average DSC improvement
of 1.96% (P < 0.001) and 1.21% (P < 0.001) over the AS decoder-only
variant (average DSC: 86.49%) and the dual-decoder variant (average DSC:
87.24%), respectively. These findings demonstrate that our MCC loss ef-
fectively enforces contextual consistency from contrastive learning among
cropped pseudo-image patches, thereby improving the generalization capa-
bility of the learned features.

4.6.3. Results of sub-component ablations on the semantics-guided segmen-
tation architecture of OESNet
To gain insights into the efficacy of the semantics-guided segmentation ar-
chitecture of the proposed OESNet, we conducted comprehensive investiga-
tions of its sub-components, i.e., the pretrained encoder weight initialization
and the SFE module. Through systematic ablation studies, we evaluated the
effects of:
• Pretrained encoder weight initialization: Random initialization
vs. transfer learning from the pretrained encoder weights.
• Semantics-guided Feature Extraction (SFE): Standard convolu-
tional blocks (two 3×3×3 convolutional layers) vs. our proposed SFE
module.
Table 6 presents the results of this ablation study. From this table, one
can observe a progressive performance degradation when cumulatively remov-
ing the key components mentioned above. Specifically, removal of the SFE
modules resulted in an average DSC of 86.60%, indicating a 1.85% perfor-
mance degradation (P < 0.001). Significant deterioration occurred when si-
multaneously disabling pretrained encoder weight initialization and the SFE
modules, yielding only 84.35% DSC, with a total performance drop of 4.10%
(P < 0.001). This sequential performance decline validates that all com-
ponents jointly enhance generalizable segmentation capability by integrating
semantic priors learned in the pretraining stage. These results validate the
efficacy of our semantics-guided segmentation architecture design.

5. Discussion
Domain-randomized image synthesis offers a promising way to eliminate
the need for real training images and support generalizable segmentation
learning. However, it remains underexplored whether such synthetic data-
driven training paradigms can be effectively applied to generalizable abdom-
inal organ segmentation without requiring domain-specific adaptation. To
mitigate the gap, we propose ReSeg, a two-stage retraining-free framework
for zero-shot segmentation of abdominal scans. Built upon two delicately
designed data engines, our proposed two-stage network first learns organ-
agnostic, generalizable semantic features, and then transitions to acquiring
abdominal-organ-specific semantic features.
We conducted comprehensive
experiments on seven abdominal segmentation datasets encompassing differ-
ent imaging modalities (CT, MRI, and PET), varying spatial resolutions,
and diverse patient demographics to evaluate the generalization capability of
ReSeg to various unseen domains. Across all evaluation datasets, our method
consistently outperformed the competing SOTA approaches, as evidenced by
the results presented in Tables 1, 2, and 3, and Figures 8, 9, 10, and 11.
To further investigate the effectiveness of the proposed GA model, the MCC
loss, and the architectural components of the proposed OESNet, we con-
ducted comprehensive ablation studies. The experimental results presented
in Tables 4, 5, and 6 provide a detailed assessment of how different compo-
nents of the proposed method contribute to the segmentation generalization
performance.
Direct application of existing domain-randomization-based segmentation
training paradigms like SynthSeg (Billot et al., 2023) to abdominal organ
segmentation proved ineffective (Tables 1, 2, and 3). Thus, we develop two
dedicated data engines with the newly designed GA model to enhance fea-
ture learning in the complex scenarios of volumetric abdominal images. In
particular, the GA model uses a dynamic lower bound for the mean parame-
ters in the GMMs to better capture the large contrast variations encountered
in abdominal imaging. This strategy significantly enhances the generaliza-
tion capability of the segmentation network, as evidenced by the improved
42


performance shown in Table 4. Our delicately designed data engines enable
generalizable segmentation learning for abdominal anatomy, which exhibit
high variations in appearance and background among patients and thus pose
significant zero-shot segmentation challenges.
Built upon the data engines, the key to the superior generalization perfor-
mance of ReSeg lies in its two-stage learning framework, which first acquires
generalizable semantic representations for diverse anatomies and then learns
organ-specific features guided by the learned semantic priors. The general-
izable semantic representation learning stage utilizes the GenRep data en-
gine, which produces paired synthetic images with diverse semantic layouts
from randomized label maps. Then, GSRNet is trained via a novel MCC
loss that encourages domain-invariant feature alignment amidst contextual
variations. This approach effectively learns domain-invariant context-aware
semantic features by leveraging multiple overlapping pseudo-image patches.
By comparison, the voxel-wise contrastive loss as proposed by Dey et al.
(2025), which relies solely on pseudo-image pairs, learns appearance-agnostic
features but overlooks contextual consistency, leading to diminished per-
formance when segmenting images with contextual changes across varying
FOVs, as confirmed by the results presented in Table 5. The subsequent
segmentation training employs the GenSeg data engine, which generates
synthetic images with anatomical spatial layouts by preserving organ po-
sition arrangements, enabling effective learning of abdominal-organ-specific
features. To gain robustness against abdominal imaging variations, OESNet
incorporates rich semantic priors derived from the well-trained GSRNet to
guide feature extraction within the SS decoder. The efficacy of such a design
is demonstrated by the results presented in Table 6.
The proposed ReSeg framework extends beyond abdominal organ seg-
mentation and can be adapted to other medical image segmentation tasks.
The pretraining stage learns organ-agnostic semantic features, which opti-
mizes GSRNet on semantically and visually diverse synthetic images, using
loss functions that focus on appearance invariance and contextual consis-
tency. This enables the network to acquire semantic features that are robust
against variations in anatomical structure, imaging contrast, resolution, and
spatial context, thereby establishing a universal representational foundation
for segmentation learning. In the subsequent segmentation learning stage,
the overall semantics-guided segmentation architecture can be applied to
new anatomical targets without being restricted to abdominal organs. In
order to adapt ReSeg for new segmentation tasks, it is sufficient to modify
43


the LP model within the dedicated data engine for segmentation learning
and then retrain OESNet. Experimental results presented in Table 7 con-
firm the effectiveness of extending ReSeg to the cardiac segmentation task,
demonstrating the generalizability of ReSeg for organ segmentation beyond
abdominal organs.
Compared to existing medical image segmentation paradigms (He et al.,
2025; Billot et al., 2023; Dey et al., 2025), the proposed method offers several
advantages. First, while most existing biomedical foundation segmentation
models (Liu et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024; He et al., 2025) rely heavily on
large-scale medical datasets covering multiple imaging domains to achieve
generalization, our proposed method benefits from the synthetic data-driven
training strategy based on domain randomization, eliminating completely
the requirement of acquiring any real training image. Second, unlike existing
domain-randomization-based segmentation training paradigms (Billot et al.,
2023; Chollet et al., 2024; Dey et al., 2024) designed for structures with
relatively fixed anatomical topology and stable tissue intensity distribution,
which yield limited segmentation performance when applied to abdominal
organs, the proposed ReSeg achieved superior abdominal organ segmenta-
tion performance, as evidenced by the results presented in Tables 1, 2, and 3.
Third, unlike the previously introduced Anatomix (Dey et al., 2025), which
is pretrained via contrastive learning on paired pseudo-images, neglecting
contextual consistency and requiring downstream finetuning on real medical
images, the proposed ReSeg combines organ-agnostic pretraining with organ-
specific segmentation training, enabling retraining-free abdominal organ seg-
mentation, which is a clear advantage for clinical deployment. Finally, when
it comes to generalizable abdominal organ segmentation, existing zero-shot
segmentation methods suffer from limited generalization capabilities, par-
ticularly under significant domain shifts, as evidenced by the experimental
results presented in Tables 1, 2, and 3. In contrast, our ReSeg demonstrates
reliable segmentation across multiple imaging modalities, even when it is
trained only on synthetic data. Notably, despite complete exclusion of real
clinical images during training, ReSeg achieves superior segmentation per-
formance against few-shot SOTA methods, including few-shot meta-learning
approaches (Ouyang et al., 2022; Tang et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025) and
finetuned representation learning models (Wu et al., 2024; Dey et al., 2025),
all of which require access to annotated real data.
44


6. Conclusion
This paper introduced ReSeg, a novel two-stage retraining-free framework
designed to achieve generalizable segmentation of abdominal organs. Specif-
ically, at the first stage, an organ-agnostic semantic representation learning
strategy was proposed, which exploited semantically and visually diverse
synthetic images generated by a delicately designed data engine to extract
semantic features robust against variations in imaging appearance and spatial
context. Such strategy endowed the pretrained network with universal repre-
sentation capabilities, laying a solid foundation for subsequent segmentation
learning. After that, at the second stage, a semantics-guided segmentation
architecture was introduced, which was optimized using diversified visual
images with anatomical spatial layouts synthesized by another delicately de-
signed data engine. Within the segmentation network, rich semantic pri-
ors derived from the pretrained network were hierarchically integrated into
the decoder, enabling effective learning of abdominal-organ-specific semantic
features. Comprehensive validation on seven abdominal datasets across CT,
MRI, and PET modalities demonstrated the superior generalization capabil-
ity of ReSeg compared to SOTA methods.
