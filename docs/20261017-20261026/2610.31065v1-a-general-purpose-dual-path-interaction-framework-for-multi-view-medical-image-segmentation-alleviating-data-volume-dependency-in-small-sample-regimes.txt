Abstract
The performance of medical image segmentation depends on a large amount of annotated
data, and most segmentation models experience a significant drop in performance in low-sample-
size scenarios. Existing semi-supervised and weakly supervised methods rely on specific network
architectures, while traditional multi-view-assisted segmentation methods require strictly paired
samples, which limits their clinical applications. To address these issues, we propose DPIF,
a general-purpose plug-and-play dual-path interaction framework compatible with arbitrary
encoder-decoder segmentation networks, which leverages cross-view anatomical complementar-
ity to reduce annotation reliance. DPIF first performs Sinkhorn optimal transport-based cross-
view distribution alignment to establish global semantic correspondence without sample-level
pairing. It then adopts conditional mutual information-guided feature decoupling to enable bidi-
rectional knowledge transfer only within the shared semantic space, suppressing view-specific
noise. Finally, persistent homology-based topological regularization encodes anatomical invari-
ants as unsupervised priors to mitigate overfitting. Experiments were conducted using 24 base-
line models on a private multicenter echocardiography dataset and two public datasets. The
results show that DPIF-enhanced models outperform single-view baseline models when trained
on comparable amounts of data. Notably, most dual-path variants trained on 40% - 60% of the
data achieve performance comparable to that of single-view models trained on the full dataset.
This demonstrates that DPIF provides a general-purpose and scalable solution for data-scarce
multi-view medical image segmentation problems.

Introduction
Accurate medical image segmentation is the foundation for clinical quantitative analysis, disease
diagnosis, and surgical planning. Although deep learning has advanced this field, its performance
still depends on large-scale datasets with pixel-level annotationsGan et al. [2025]. Unlike natural
images, annotating medical images requires specialized clinical knowledge, making the process both
time-consuming and costly. For multi-view imaging protocols, each view must be annotated sepa-
rately, leading to a significant increase in workload and costs. Therefore, the scarcity of annotated
data remains a major bottleneck for clinical translation, particularly for rare diseasesDissanayake
et al. [2025].
In standard clinical practice, images of the same anatomical structure are typically acquired
from multiple angles to enable a comprehensive assessment of lesionsBerahmand et al. [2025]. For
example, a standard echocardiogram simultaneously acquires both the long-axis and short-axis
views of the left ventricle. Although these views contain highly complementary anatomical infor-
mation, they differ significantly in terms of geometric structure and imaging characteristicsDu et al.
[2026]. Current mainstream segmentation methods still process each view independently, failing
to fully leverage the inherent structural and semantic relationships between views. Most exist-
ing multi-view fusion methods employ simple strategiesLiu et al. [2024b], Majern´ık et al. [2026].
These methods overlook the substantial spatial structural differences and variations in semantic
distribution across views, often introducing redundant noise and yielding only limited performance
improvements. More critically, most multi-view frameworks are tightly coupled with specific back-
bone architectures, which hinders their rapid adaptation to new tasks and poses challenges for
cross-scenario deploymentZhou et al. [2026].
To address these fundamental limitations, we propose a general-purpose dual-path interaction
framework. Its core idea is to leverage the complementarity of cross-view anatomical structures
as an alternative to expanding the annotated dataset. The framework features a universal, plug-
and-play design that is compatible with various segmentation backbone models and achieves high-
precision segmentation with only a small number of annotated samples. The main contributions of
this study are summarized as follows:
We propose a general-purpose dual-path interaction framework designed to reduce reliance on
segmented annotated data. This framework can be integrated into any encoder-decoder Segmen-
tation network. We propose a cross-view alignment mechanism based on optimal transport theory.
It establishes semantic correspondences by minimizing the Wasserstein distance, thereby enabling
distribution-level alignment of unpaired data.
We propose a two-layer decoupling strategy for
cross-view knowledge transfer. Under conditional mutual information constraints, features are de-
composed into shared and view-specific components, and knowledge transfer and enhancement are
performed only in the shared space to suppress noise propagation. We propose a regularization
method based on cross-view topological consistency. This method uses anatomical topological in-
variants as shared prior information to impose constraints, thereby narrowing the hypothesis space
and mitigating the problem of overfitting in small-sample settings.

Related WORK
2.1
Multi-view Medical Image Segmentation
Existing multi-view medical image segmentation methods can be broadly categorized into three
paradigms: input-level, feature-level, and decision-level fusion.
Input-level fusion stacks multi-
view images as multi-channel inputs but ignores spatial differences between views. Feature-level
2


fusion directly concatenates or superimposes intermediate features within the network, but carries
the risk of introducing view-specific noise and artifactsWang and Voiculescu [2022]. Decision-level
fusion, while capable of merging independent prediction outputs, discards complementary semantic
information present in the hidden layersLi et al. [2025]. Although improvements based on attention
mechanisms have shown some promise, most fusion schemes remain constrained by their network
architectures, making it difficult to transfer them across different segmentation pipelinesZhan et al.
[2023]. Furthermore, most of these schemes are designed for fully annotated datasets and perform
poorly when data is limited. Crucially, nearly all existing methods require strictly paired multi-view
patient data, which imposes severe constraints on real-world clinical inputs.
2.2
Small-Sample Medical Image Segmentation
To address the issue of scarce annotations, existing research primarily follows three approaches:
semi-supervised learning, weakly supervised learning, and few-shot learning. Semi-supervised meth-
ods utilize large amounts of unlabeled data to aid training, but still rely on a certain scale of
unlabeled data, thus imposing requirements on the data volumeCao et al. [2025]. Weakly super-
vised methods employ weak labels, such as point annotations and bounding box annotations, to
reduce annotation costs. However, due to the limitations imposed by the simplicity of these la-
bels, there is an upper bound on accuracy.Chen et al. [2025a] Few-shot learning methods aim to
achieve generalization with small datasets through cross-domain knowledge transfer. However, due
to significant variations across medical imaging domains, their practical effectiveness remains uncer-
tainDissanayake et al. [2025]. It is worth noting that existing methods rarely exploit the inherent
correlations among multi-view images of the same object, nor have they established a universal
interaction scheme that can be reused across models. Meanwhile, the cost of acquiring multi-view
images in clinical settings is far lower than the cost of annotating data required for a single view,
and this potential has yet to be fully utilized.
3
Method
3.1
Overview
As shown in Fig 1, our proposed Dual-Path Interaction Framework (DPIF) adopts a symmetric
dual-branch architecture, in which the backbone networks share the same structure but are initial-
ized independently. Each branch receives a single-view image for segmenting the target anatomical
structure.
The training process is divided into three stages.
In the feature encoding stage, a
dual-branch encoder extracts multiscale features.
Subsequently, the ODI module is invoked at
designated encoder-decoder layers to perform feature decoupling, unpaired distribution alignment,
and adaptive bidirectional feature enhancement, after which the enhanced features are fed back to
the decoder. Meanwhile, a topological regularization loss serves as an anatomical prior to ensure
topological consistency across different views. In the final decoding and joint training stage, each
decoder upsamples and aggregates the features to generate segmentation outputs and soft proba-
bility maps. The entire framework is optimized via a composite loss function. During the inference
stage, the interaction module is disabled, and the two branches operate independently.

3.2.1
Two-Layer Feature Decoupling
Multi-view medical images contain both shared anatomical semantics and view-specific noise, in-
tensity shifts, and artifacts. Directly propagating raw features across branches reduces the quality
of feature interaction, especially when data is limited. To address this issue, our proposed two-layer
feature decoupling method decomposes the input features of each view into two mutually exclusive
components: a shared semantic component for cross-view information exchange, and a view-specific
component that is excluded from inter-branch interaction. Each branch employs two parallel 1 × 1
convolutional encoders. One encoder, with shared weights, extracts shared semantic features F c
to construct a unified semantic space, while the view-independent encoder generates view-specific
features F p:

3.3
Cross-View Topological Consistency Regularization Loss
In small-sample scenarios, limited training coverage may induce spurious pixel-level correlations and
produce unreasonable segmentation results, while pixel-based loss functions struggle to mitigate
this phenomenon. We consider that anatomical structures possess inherent topological invariance,
and that key topological statistics across multi-view projections exhibit high consistency, which
can serve as unsupervised priors to narrow the model’s hypothesis space and alleviate overfitting.
Therefore, drawing inspiration from persistent homology-based topological data analysis methods,
we propose a cross-view topological consistency regularization lossKapulkin and Kershaw [2026].
Starting from the segmentation probability map of each view, we obtain a persistent diagram
(PD) through sub-level set filtering, where the points encode topological features characterized
by thresholds and corresponding persistent values. Higher persistence values indicate more stable
and anatomically plausible structures. Since the exact persistence homology calculation is non-
differentiable, we employ a differentiable approximation method: applying multiscale Gaussian
smoothing to the probability graph to obtain an approximate persistence graph. The second-order
Wasserstein distance between the persistence graphs of the two views forms our topological loss
function:

4
Experiment
4.1
Datasets
We conducted our evaluation using three multi-view medical imaging datasets, including a private
multicenter echocardiography dataset (referred to as PME), the publicly available FLARE2023
liver CT dataset, and the MSD hippocampal MRI dataset. The specific datasets used are shown
in Fig 2.
PME Dataset: This dataset compiles data from 429 patients across five clinical centers, pro-
viding images of the apical four-chamber long-axis view and the papillary muscle-level short-axis
view, along with annotations of the left ventricular myocardium. One image at the end of systole
and one at the end of diastole are selected for each cardiac cycle, resulting in 858 images per view.
FLARE2023 DatasetMa et al. [2024]: 2D slices with liver annotations from two views were
extracted from the 3D abdominal CT volumes. After filtering based on liver size and the presence
of tumors, 581 images were retained for each view for 581 patients.
MSD DatasetSimpson et al. [2019]: We extracted 2D sections with dual views labeled for the
anterior hippocampus. After image quality screening, 172 images were retained for each view for
172 patients.
To distinguish between the two views, the results are uniformly labeled as the “long-axis view”
and the “short-axis view.” All datasets were split at the patient level to prevent data leaks. First,
20% of the data was selected as an independent test set (not used for training), and the remaining
data was subjected to five-fold cross-validation. All datasets underwent a standardized preprocess-
ing workflow. For each dataset, images were cropped to a size of 512×512 pixels. During training,
data augmentation was applied only to the training set to improve the model’s generalization ability.
Neither the validation set nor the test set underwent data augmentation to ensure the objectivity
and consistency of the evaluation.
4.2
Evaluation Metrics
We adopted four commonly used evaluation metrics in medical image segmentation: The Dice
similarity coefficient(DSC), which measures the degree of overlap between the segmentation results
and the gold standard. The Jaccard index (IoU), which is the ratio of the intersection to the union.
The 95% Hausdorff distance (HD95), which measures the degree of alignment of the segmentation
boundaries. The average surface distance (ASD), which is the average distance between points
on the segmentation boundaries. Using 5-fold cross-validation, we identified five optimal models,
tested them on the independent test set, and reported the average values of the aforementioned
metrics.
4.3
Experimental Setup
We implemented all networks using the PyTorch 1.12 framework. The Adam optimizer was used
with an initial learning rate of 2e-4, which was reduced to a minimum learning rate of 1e-5 using
a cosine annealing strategy. The batch size was set to 2, with a total of 100 training epochs. The
backbone network was frozen for the first 30 epochs. The similarity threshold was set to τ = 0.2,
the distillation temperature to T = 2.0, and the loss weights to α = 0.1, βs = 0.05, βl = 0.05.
Except for specific parameters, the settings for the comparative experiments were identical to those
of our method. All experiments were conducted on two NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs.

5.1
Comparison with Single-Path Segmentation Models
To validate the DPIF’s generality and its advantages in few-shot scenarios, we selected 24 represen-
tative encoder-decoder networks as single-path (SP) baseline models and constructed corresponding
dual-path (DP) variants by integrating the DPIF framework. All models were trained and eval-
uated on three multi-view datasets. The complete quantitative results are shown in TABLE 1 to
TABLE 3 and Fig 3. Experimental results show that all dual-path variants achieved performance
improvements on the three datasets. In terms of the DSC metric, the dual-path variants achieved
an average improvement of 13.33% on the long-axis views and 8.97% on the short-axis views across
the three datasets. Notably, the hippocampus long-axis dataset showed the largest average DSC
improvement, reaching 19.2%. Among these, RollingUNet demonstrated the most significant im-
provement, with a 57.57% increase in DSC on the hippocampus long-axis dataset. This confirms
that DPIF can effectively leverage the complementary anatomical information inherent in both
views without introducing directional performance bias. Regarding boundary accuracy metrics,
the average HD95 decreased by 4.27 in the long-axis view and by 2.38 in the short-axis view across
the three datasets. It is worth noting that a few models (such as SCSEUNet) showed a slight
decrease in HD95. Nevertheless, the overall trend confirms that DPIF effectively enhances the
boundary continuity and anatomical fidelity of segmentation results for most architectures, deliv-
ering stable and consistent performance improvements across a variety of segmentation network
architectures.

Comparison with Multi-View Segmentation Methods
We selected five representative multi-view medical image segmentation methods for comparison. To
ensure fairness in the experiments, all comparison methods used the same UMNet as the backbone
network, were trained on exactly the same data split. The quantitative results are shown in TABLE
4. Fig 4 visualizes the percentage improvement for all methods. It can be seen that the DPIF
framework demonstrates a consistent performance advantage across all views on the three datasets.
In particular, on the long-axis view of the liver dataset, DP-UMNet achieves a 5.77% improvement
over the single-path baseline, significantly outperforming all comparison methods. In contrast, on
all three test datasets, all other methods underperform DP-UMNet. Most of these methods even
performed worse than the single-path UMNet baseline model, with only a few achieving a slight
improvement on the liver dataset. Among them, MSMVNet and CoBioNet exhibited the most
significant performance declines. Overall, compared to existing multi-view segmentation methods,
the DPIF framework achieves stable and consistent performance improvements in multi-view tasks
spanning different imaging modalities and anatomical structures.

5.3
Ablation Studies and Analyses
To quantitatively verify individual contributions and synergies among DPIF components, we per-
form progressive ablation experiments on the multicenter PME echocardiography dataset with
UMNet as the backbone. Taking single-path UMNet as baseline, nine ablation variants are built to
evaluate four modules: two-layer feature decoupling (A), Sinkhorn unpaired distribution alignment
(B), adaptive interaction weights (C), and cross-view topological consistency regularization (D).
Quantitative results are reported in TABLE 5. The results show that decoupling features across
two layers yields only a slight improvement, as it achieves feature separation and noise suppression
within branches but does not facilitate cross-view information exchange. Sinkhorn-based distri-
bution alignment is the primary driver of performance improvement, increasing DSC by 2% for
the long-axis view and 2.24% for the short-axis view, respectively. This validates that distribution-
level semantic matching is the primary channel for cross-view knowledge transfer. When used alone,
adaptive interaction weights yielded a negligible improvement, indicating that dynamic weighting
cannot function effectively without the support of distribution alignment. Cross-view topological
regularization independently optimized segmentation boundaries, increasing the long-axis DSC by
1.14% and reducing HD95 by approximately 0.39.
5.4
Analysis of Different Training Data Usage
To investigate the performance and few-shot adaptability of DPIF under different levels of data
usage, we conducted gradient data experiments on three multi-view datasets using three represen-
tative backbone networks (RollingUNet, UNet and UMNet). Among these, RollingUNet represents
a class of networks with poor baseline performance, UNet represents a class of classic segmentation
networks, and UMNet represents a class of networks with superior baseline performance. We em-
ployed a stratified inclusive sampling method, stratified by multicenter origin and lesion proportion,
to construct training subsets of 20%, 40%, 60%, and 80%. Smaller subsets were fully nested within
larger ones to isolate data volume as the sole variable while preserving the statistical distribution
of the full dataset. All experiments used a fixed test set and five-fold cross-validation. The cor-
responding performance curves are illustrated in Fig 5. The results show that the performance
of the dual-path model increases monotonically as the amount of training data increases.
The
performance improvement is most significant in the range from 20% to 40%. In most cases, the
DPIF-enhanced model can achieve the same accuracy as the full-data single-path model even when
the annotation rate is below 60For weaker backbone networks, DPIF delivers greater performance
improvements. DP-RollingUNet, trained on only 20% of the labeled data, outperformed the full-
14


data single-path baseline model on the PME short-axis view. Cross-modal analysis indicates that
the benefits of few-shot learning are positively correlated with task difficulty. In the PME echocar-
diography task, DP-UMNet outperforms full-data single-path models with a 60% annotation rate.
On the high-contrast FLARE2023 liver CT dataset, it requires only 40% annotated data. On a
high-resolution hippocampal MRI dataset, DP-UMNet requires a 60% annotation rate to approach
the performance of the full-data single-path model.
5.5
Blind Review of Segmentation Results by Clinicians
To verify whether the improvements achieved through dual-pathway interaction could translate into
clinically usable segmentation results, we conducted a blinded review study across three datasets.
Three ultrasound specialists and three radiology specialists evaluated the segmentation results
using a 1–5-point Likert scale for 30 randomly selected cases from each view. For each case, the
original image and the segmentation results from both DP-UMNet and SP-UMNet were displayed
simultaneously, with the positions of the results randomized to avoid subjective bias. The results
are shown in Table6. The advantages of DP-UMNet were most pronounced in the sections with
the weakest baseline performance: in the PME short-axis section, DP-UMNet raised the average
score to 3.49 ± 0.60, and the proportion of cases with a score of ≥4 increased from 24.4% to
52.2%. In cases where single-path baseline models had already achieved high clinical scores (e.g.,
FLARE2023 short-axis and MSD short-axis), the dual-path enhancement did not yield further
significant improvements. The inter-reader consistency (ICC) across views ranged from moderate
to high. These results confirm that the introduction of DPIF not only improves the agreement
between segmentation results and ground-truth labels but also demonstrates that DPIF achieves
consistent clinical validation.

Discussion
In the comparative experiment, traditional multi-view methods cannot guarantee consistent perfor-
mance improvements and sometimes even underperform compared to single-view baseline models,
particularly on noisier, unpaired data. Thanks to its multiple functional modules—particularly
feature alignment and two-layer decoupling, DPIF mitigates the effects of noise and differences in
interaction targets. As a result, it continues to demonstrate performance improvements even on
challenging datasets such as ultrasound and MRI.
Gradient data experiments confirm that DPIF improves data utilization efficiency. The mag-
nitude of performance improvement increases as task difficulty and baseline model performance
decrease. For low-performance backbone networks such as RollingUNet, DPIF delivers significant
improvements, indicating its immense potential in the field of rare diseases where annotations are
scarce. Notably, performance continues to improve steadily even when the data usage is 100%.

Therefore, DPIF serves not only as a solution for few-shot learning but also as a general-purpose
enhancement module for fully supervised multi-view clinical pipelines.
Nevertheless, the framework presented in this paper has some limitations. The current imple-
mentation supports only two-view interactions, extending it to three or more views would facilitate
the volumetric segmentation of 3D data. Topological regularization employs a differentiable persis-
tent homology approximation, which may overlook subtle topological features. Therefore, a more
precise differentiable topological solver is needed.
7
Conclusion
This paper proposes a general-purpose, plug-and-play dual-path interaction framework called DPIF
for multi-view medical image segmentation, which improved data utilization when data is limited.
DPIF achieves consistent performance improvements across 24 encoder-decoder backbone networks
and outperforms existing multi-view segmentation methods. It can match or exceed the perfor-
mance of single-path models trained on the full dataset using only a subset of data of a certain
size. This demonstrates that DPIF provides a practical and scalable solution for multi-view medical
image segmentation under data-scarce conditions.
