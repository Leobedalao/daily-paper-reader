O2D-MedNet: An Optimization-Aware
Dual-Domain Framework for Robust Medical

Shipeng Hu, Miaohui Wang, Jing Qin, Sukun Tian, and Yuchun Sun
Abstract—Medical image segmentation (MIS) remains challenging due to composite degradations and multi-scale geometric variations,
which jointly impair lesion-background separability, structural consistency, and optimization stability. Existing single-stream frameworks
entangle heterogeneous cues within a single representation pathway, while hybrid-stream approaches often rely on generic or static
fusion, limiting effective coordination across domains and failing to explicitly address optimization instability. To overcome these
limitations, we propose a new optimization-aware dual-domain framework for MIS (O2D-MedNet). First, a degradation-aware dual-
domain interaction (DDI) module integrates Fourier- and Wavelet-domain features to suppress degradation-sensitive responses while
preserving structural and fine-detail information. Second, a cross-backbone contour-guided fusion (CGF) module enables selective
interaction between CNN-based local detail modeling and visual state-space-based global structure modeling, improving boundary
accuracy and representation consistency. Third, a gradient-context optimization (GCO) strategy stabilizes training by adaptively adjusting
learning dynamics based on loss trends and gradient statistics, mitigating instability caused by class imbalance and heterogeneous
sample difficulty. Extensive experiments on six benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over state-of-the-art MIS methods
across multiple imaging modalities. Further analyses confirm enhanced sensitivity, structural consistency, and optimization stability,
validating the effectiveness of the proposed O2D-MedNet for robust MIS under heterogeneous conditions.
Index Terms—Medical image segmentation, dual-domain modeling, cross-backbone fusion, optimization stability

We argue that a fundamental limitation of existing MIS
frameworks lies in the coupled treatment of degradation
suppression, representation learning, and optimization. In
most existing MIS approaches, these factors are implic-
itly handled within a unified learning process, making it
difficult to dynamically balance feature restoration, local-
global representation coordination, and optimization stabil-
ity. Consequently, MIS performance often deteriorates in the
presence of composite degradations, large anatomical varia-
tions, class imbalance, and heterogeneous sample difficulty.
To address these challenges, we introduce a new
optimization-aware dual-domain framework for MIS (O2D-
MedNet). The proposed method follows a progressive design
principle that explicitly decouples degradation suppression,
representation learning, and optimization dynamics. First,
a dual-domain interaction module exploits complementary
Fourier- and Wavelet-domain information to enhance feature
discriminability. Second, a contour-guided cross-backbone
fusion mechanism facilitates selective integration of local
and global representations, mitigating redundant and noisy
feature propagation. Third, a gradient-context-aware op-
timization strategy dynamically adapts learning behavior
according to loss trends and gradient statistics, improv-
ing optimization stability under heterogeneous data condi-
tions. Extensive experiments on six benchmark segmenta-
tion datasets spanning three medical imaging categories as
well as diverse lesions and anatomical structures demon-
strate the effectiveness and generalization of the proposed
MIS framework.
The main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose O2D-MedNet, an optimization-aware dual-
domain cross-backbone interaction framework that ex-
plicitly decouples degradation suppression, representa-
tion learning, and training dynamics, enabling robust
MIS under composite degradations and multi-scale
anatomical variations.
• We introduce a degradation-aware dual-domain inter-
action (DDI) mechanism that exploits complementary
Fourier- and Wavelet-domain representations to improve
lesion–background separability while preserving both
structural integrity and fine-grained details.
• We design a cross-backbone contour-guided fusion
(CGF) module that adaptively integrates CNN-based
local features with state-space-based global representa-
tions, enhancing structural consistency across complex
anatomical morphologies and scale variations.
• We develop a stable gradient-context optimization
(GCO) strategy that modulates optimization behavior
according to loss dynamics and gradient statistics, im-
proving optimization stability and training robustness
under class imbalance and heterogeneous sample diffi-
culty.

MIS has been widely studied, with recent advances largely
driven by deep neural networks. From the perspective of
network architecture, existing MIS methods can be broadly

2.1
Reference Baselines
We first review several representative reference baselines
that provide distinct architectural paradigms for image
segmentation. DeepLabv3+ [16] integrates atrous spatial
pyramid pooling with an encoder–decoder architecture
to capture multi-scale contextual information and pro-
gressively refine object boundaries. U2-Net [17] adopts a
nested U-shaped architecture composed of residual U-
blocks, enabling the joint modeling of fine-grained local
details and multi-scale contextual features. In contrast, Med-
SAM [18] exemplifies the promptable medical foundation-
model paradigm. It employs a ViT-based image encoder
to extract image representations and a prompt encoder to
embed input prompts, which are subsequently integrated
by the mask decoder to generate segmentation masks.
These methods represent three complementary architectural
paradigms, ranging from conventional encoder-decoder
networks and nested multi-scale architectures to prompt-
conditioned foundation MIS models.
2.2
Single-stream Frameworks
Single-stream MIS frameworks [29], [30], [31] rely on a
dominant network pathway, where feature extraction, con-
text aggregation, and prediction are conducted on a sin-
gle backbone architecture, including U-shaped- or CNN-
based, Transformer-based, visual state-space model (VSSM)-
based, and other domain-based models.
U-shaped or CNN-based. Early MIS methods are primarily
based on U-shaped encoder–decoder or CNN-based net-
works, where hierarchical convolutional features are pro-
gressively encoded and decoded for dense prediction [32],
[33]. Representative models, such as ACNN [19], DRINet
[20], BCDU-Net [21], CyCoSeg [6], FusionNet [34], UNeXt
[35], MBP-SSNet [36], and EAG-SAE-ESTF [11], enhance this
paradigm through improved skip connections, contextual
modeling, and feature refinement. These methods are effec-
tive at capturing local textures and boundary details, which
are essential for segmenting small lesions and thin anatomi-
cal structures. However, their convolution-dominant design
inherently relies on local receptive fields, making it difficult
to model long-range dependencies and maintain global
structural coherence when anatomical variations are large
or spatially dispersed.

cently, SAM2-UNet [27] integrates the Hiera backbone of
SAM2 with a U-shaped decoder and lightweight adapters
for prompt-free segmentation, retaining a dominant single-
stream pathway. While these approaches improve the repre-
sentation of large-scale structures, they still operate within
a single pathway, where local detail preservation and global
structure modeling are tightly coupled. This coupling makes
it difficult to adaptively prioritize boundary-sensitive cues
or global semantics under varying imaging conditions. Es-
pecially, global aggregation may smooth fine structures or
weaken subtle lesion boundaries in low-contrast or noisy
regions.
VSSM-based. More recently, VSSM-based architectures,
such as Mamba-UNet [23], LKM-UNet [24], H-vmuNet [39],
UltraLight VM [40], and Mamba-Sea [25], provide an alter-
native mechanism for efficient long-range modeling. These
methods improve computational efficiency while maintain-
ing global structure awareness. However, similar to CNN-
and Transformer-based MIS schemes, they still encode local
details, global structures, and scale variations within a single
pathway. Consequently, they continue to face an inherent
trade-off between local sensitivity and global coherence, es-
pecially under composite degradations and heterogeneous
anatomical variations.

fusion strategies. Consequently, degradation suppression is

Summary. Despite their diverse network architectures,
single-stream MIS methods fundamentally entangle degra-
dation suppression, representation learning, and optimiza-
tion within a unified or single pathway. This implicit
coupling assumes that a single representation can simul-
taneously resolve degradation-induced ambiguity, capture
multi-scale structural variations, or maintain stable op-
timization dynamics. However, such an assumption be-
comes weak or invalid under heterogeneous imaging condi-
tions, where these factors impose competing and condition-
dependent requirements. As a result, single-stream MIS
models lack the flexibility to adaptively prioritize local de-
tail, global structure, or robustness to degradations, leading
to compromised segmentation performance.

Hybrid-frequency-based. Hybrid frequencies [43], [44] pro-

the representation bottleneck by introducing multiple path-

stream interaction, and optimization as loosely coupled
components. Their fusion strategies often rely on static
or weakly adaptive mechanisms, implicitly assuming that
complementary representations can be combined without
considering condition-dependent dominance or degrada-
tion sensitivity. Moreover, learning optimization remains
external to network architectures, limiting the ability to
stabilize training. As a result, although hybrid-stream mod-
els improve representation diversity, they still lack a uni-
fied and adaptive mechanism to coordinate degradation
suppression, representation interaction, and optimization
dynamics under challenging conditions.

METHODOLOGY
The overall architecture of O2D-MedNet is illustrated in
Fig. 2. The framework consists of three main components:
(1) a degradation-aware dual-domain interaction (DDI)
module, (2) a cross-backbone contour-guided fusion (CGF)
module, and (3) a stable gradient-context optimization
(GCO) strategy. Given an input medical image, DDI first
enhances feature discriminability through complementary
Fourier- and Wavelet-domain processing. The refined fea-
tures are subsequently encoded by parallel CNN and VSSM
branches, where CGF enables selective fusion between local
and global representations. During training, GCO dynam-
ically adapts optimization behavior according to gradient
context to improve optimization stability.
3.1
Degradation-Aware Dual-Domain Interaction
In MIS, composite degradations are often amplified in
early feature representations, leading to ambiguous le-
sion–background boundaries and unstable downstream
learning [48]. Since such degradation-induced responses
cannot be reliably suppressed by representation learning
alone, we first introduce a dual-domain refinement stage
to improve feature separability before high-level modeling.

tion address different levels of degradation uncertainty, as
shown in Fig. 3. Meanwhile, composite degradations often
vary substantially across samples: some images are dom-
inated by specular highlights, whereas others are mainly
affected by noise, artifacts, or weak contrast. Therefore, the
scalar gate gb estimates the global reliability of dual-domain
responses for each sample, which is broadcast to all chan-
nel and spatial positions during fusion. However, lesions
and anatomical structures are also spatially non-uniform
within the same sample. A spatial modulation step is thus
required to suppress local false responses while preserving
informative lesion regions. In this sense, gb determines how
much dual-domain information should be trusted globally,
whereas the spatial gate refines where such information
should be emphasized locally.
In the DDI module, we further introduce dual-domain
dynamic interaction to enhance spatial complementarity.

3.2
Cross-Backbone Contour-Guided Fusion
After dual-domain feature interaction, the model proceeds
to representation learning through parallel CNN and VSSM
branches. It is noted that CNN features are sensitive to
local textures and boundary details, and VSSM features
are effective for modeling long-range dependencies and
global semantics. However, directly fusing these heteroge-
neous representations assumes feature compatibility, which
is often violated under complex degradations and scale
variations [49].

3.3
Stable Gradient-Context Optimization
Even with improved feature representations, fixed or em-
pirically designed learning rate schemes are insufficient to
ensure fast convergence and stable optimization in complex
degradations [50]. To address this issue, we propose the
GCO strategy that treats hyperparameter scheduling as
an adaptive decision process. At the end of epoch t, the
controller observes state st, selects action at, and maps it
to the learning rate lrt+1 for the next epoch. After training
epoch t + 1, it receives reward rt, observes the next state
st+1, and updates the Q-table Q(st, at).

EXPERIMENTAL VALIDATIONS
In the experiments, we evaluate three aspects of O2D-
MedNet: (1) the robustness of MIS performance under het-
erogeneous degradations, (2) the effectiveness of explicit
local-global decoupling with selective cross-backbone inter-
action, and (3) the impact of optimization-aware training
on optimization stability. Specifically, we have conducted
comprehensive evaluations across various heterogeneous
medical imaging datasets, diverse segmentation backbones,
and extensive ablation settings, together with computational
complexity analysis.
4.1
Experimental Protocols
4.1.1
Datasets
We evaluate O2D-MedNet on six benchmark datasets span-
ning three imaging modalities and diverse anatomical struc-
tures, including optical RGB images (Oral [51] and Kvasir-SEG
[52]), radiological images (COVID-19 [53] and MLUA [54]),
and ultrasound images (BUSI [55] and CT2US [56]).
• Oral: An in-house dataset with 1,278 expert-annotated oral
mucosal RGB images, characterized by small, scattered
lesions and strong specular highlights.
• Kvasir-SEG: A polyp segmentation dataset with 1,000 en-
doscopic RGB images, featuring large variations in lesion
scale, shape, and illumination.
• COVID-19: A collection of 2,729 annotated CT images
aggregated from three public datasets, exhibiting low
contrast, blurred boundaries, and imaging artifacts.
• MLUA: A dental panoramic X-ray image dataset for
caries segmentation containing 2,389 images, charac-
terized by subtle contrast differences and complex le-
sion–background boundaries.
• CT2US: A dataset with 4,586 kidney segmentation ultra-
sound image samples, where synthesized ultrasound-like
appearance introduces complex texture variations.
• BUSI: A breast ultrasound image dataset containing 780
images from 600 patients, with severe speckle noise and
intensity inhomogeneity.
The six datasets exhibit substantial differences in fore-
ground scale and object scale, resulting in significant
changes in distribution across datasets. The heterogeneity
of these datasets can effectively validate the robustness
of subsequent comparison approaches. As shown in Fig.

4.1.2
Evaluation Metrics
We adopt Dice similarity coefficient (DSC, Dice) [61], mean
intersection-over-union (mIoU) [62], and Sensitivity [63] to
measure region overlap and foreground detection capability,
which have been widely used in existing MIS frameworks
[18], [64], [65]. In addition, Betti Error (BE) [66] is widely
used to evaluate topological consistency by measuring the
discrepancy between predicted and ground-truth Betti num-
bers. For multiclass settings, Dice and mIoU are macro-
averaged over foreground classes excluding the lesion-
background, and for binary settings, all metrics are com-
puted on the foreground class.

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
This paper presents an optimization-aware dual-domain
network for robust medical image segmentation (O2D-
MedNet) under composite degradations and multi-scale ge-
ometric variations. The proposed framework is based on
the observation that degradation suppression, local-global
representation learning, and optimization stability are in-
herently coupled and should be addressed in a coordi-
nated manner. To this end, O2D-MedNet integrates dual-
domain feature refinement, contour-guided cross-backbone
interaction, and gradient-context-aware optimization within
a unified framework. Extensive experiments on six bench-
mark datasets spanning multiple imaging modalities and
anatomical targets demonstrate consistent improvements
in segmentation accuracy, sensitivity, and topological con-
sistency, highlighting the effectiveness of jointly modeling
feature representation and optimization dynamics.
Despite these promising results, several limitations re-
main. The current study is restricted to 2D segmentation
tasks, and extending the framework to 3D volumes and
temporal imaging data requires further investigation. In ad-
dition, resizing inputs to a fixed resolution may compromise
extremely fine-grained structures, while the dual-backbone
design introduces additional parameters and inference la-
tency compared with lightweight architectures. Although
improved topological metrics indicate enhanced structural
consistency, further clinical validation is necessary to assess
practical utility in real-world settings. Future work will
focus on extending the framework to 3D and longitudinal
imaging scenarios, improving robustness under limited an-
notations and cross-center domain shifts, and incorporating
uncertainty-aware modeling to further enhance reliability
and generalization.
