Abstract
Medical image segmentation is crucial for clinical diagnosis and treatment
planning, requiring both global contextual understanding and fine-grained
structural precision.
While the Segment Anything Model (SAM) demon-
strates strong generalization capabilities, its direct application to medical im-
ages often lacks domain-specific adaptability. Meanwhile, State Space Models
(SSMs)-based methods, such as Mamba, efficiently capture long-range depen-
dencies but may struggle with preserving fine anatomical details. To address
these challenges, we propose a segment anything model based context guided
mamba unet, SAMBA-UNet, a novel framework that integrates SAM’s con-
text knowledge with Mamba’s hierarchical feature learning to enhance both
global representation and local detail refinement. Specifically, we introduce
the Hierarchical Context Enhancement Module (HCEM), an attention-based
fusion module that deeply incorporates the multi-level features of SAM and
Mamba to improve detail perception and segmentation precision. Addition-
ally, we employ LoRA and Adapter-based fine-tuning to adapt SAM to med-
ical imaging tasks, further enhancing its domain-specific segmentation capa-
bility and robustness. We conducted a comprehensive study on several med-
ical segmentation datasets, including Synapse, ISIC17, and ISIC18, evaluat-
ing our method across multiple metrics. Experimental results demonstrate
that SAMBA-UNet achieves state-of-the-art performance, effectively balanc-
ing generalization, segmentation accuracy, and computational efficiency. Our

1. Introduction
Medical image segmentation plays a crucial role in various clinical ap-
plications, including CT and MRI analysis, enabling precise extraction of
pathological regions such as tumors and lesions. This aids early disease de-
tection and accurate diagnosis[3, 4]. However, unlike natural images, medical
images often exhibit complex anatomical structures and overlapping patho-
logical tissues, making segmentation a challenging task. Traditional CNN-
based models like U-Net[12] excel in capturing local texture and edge fea-
tures, yet their limited receptive fields often fail to incorporate broader spa-
tial relationships essential for accurate region delineation. To address this
limitation, Transformer-based architectures introduced self-attention mech-
anisms capable of modeling global dependencies, enhancing their ability to
integrate distant anatomical cues. However, their quadratic computational
complexity significantly increases the burden when processing high-resolution
or volumetric (3D) medical image data, often rendering them impractical for
clinical deployment due to memory and inference constraints
In response, State Space Model (SSM) architectures, particularly the
Mamba family, have emerged as an efficient and context-aware alternative.
Inspired by structured state-space sequence models (e.g., S4), Mamba ap-
plies these principles to visual data, enabling long-range dependency mod-
eling with linear complexity, while also being GPU-hardware-aware. In the
medical domain, hybrid CNN–SSM networks like U-Mamba combine convo-
lutional layers for local texture and Mamba blocks for global context, demon-
strating robust scalability across various imaging modalities. Pure SSM net-
works such as VM-UNet and SegMamba further push this model paradigm
by leveraging Visual State Space modules or multi-directional Mamba blocks
to process dense volumetric data efficiently and coherently. A recent survey
even reports that Mamba-based architectures outperform traditional trans-
former and CNN approaches in high-resolution image tasks while requiring
fewer resources.
The Segment Anything Model (SAM), as a foundation model trained on
large-scale natural image datasets, exhibits strong generalization and zero-
2


shot segmentation capabilities.
Its prompt-driven architecture allows for
flexible object delineation and cross-domain adaptability.
However, when
applied directly to medical imaging, SAM often fails to accurately capture
low-contrast anatomical boundaries or subtle pathological regions, due to
domain-specific discrepancies in semantics and texture distribution. In con-
trast, Mamba-style State Space Models (SSMs) have shown great promise in
medical image segmentation by efficiently modeling long-range dependencies
with linear computational complexity. These models are particularly effective
at capturing spatial continuity and structural context across volumetric data,
yet they lack the rich semantic priors that foundation models like SAM pro-
vide—limiting their performance in cases of ambiguous or poorly contrasted
anatomy. These observations suggest that SAM and Mamba offer highly com-
plementary strengths: the former provides global semantic awareness, while
the latter excels at structural consistency and context reasoning. A unified
framework combining the two can potentially overcome the respective lim-
itations of each, enabling segmentation models that are both semantically
informed and anatomically precise. Despite this promising direction, prior
research has mostly focused on either adapting SAM to medical domains
or refining Mamba-based architectures independently. To date, no studies
have systematically integrated SAM’s learned representations with Mamba’s
context modeling to address the unique challenges of medical image segmen-
tation—a gap this work aims to fill.
To bridge this gap, we propose SAMamba-UNet, a hybrid Mamba-based
model that integrates the powerful visual representation learning of SAM
with the long-range contextual modeling of Mamba. Our approach employs
joint fine-tuning of SAM via LoRA[25] and Adapter[26] techniques to adapt
it for medical image segmentation. Extensive experiments on Synapse[44],
ISIC17[42], and ISIC18[43] datasets demonstrate that SAMamba-UNet achieves
competitive performance across various medical segmentation tasks, includ-
ing organ and skin lesion segmentation. In summary, our primary contribu-
tions are as follows:
1) We present SAMBA-UNet, a novel framework that integrates SAM’s
contextual knowledge with Mamba’s hierarchical feature learning. By
leveraging SAM’s generalization ability and Mamba’s long-range mod-
eling, our method enhances global context understanding and fine-
grained anatomical segmentation, achieving superior performance in
medical image segmentation.

2) We designed a Hierarchical Context Enhancement Module (HCEM),
a novel attention-based fusion module by deep fusion of SAM and

3) We concurrently collaborative fine-tune SAM with the incorporation of
both LoRA and Adapter to learn medical-specific knowledge, further

2. Related works
2.1. Deep Neural Architectures for Medical Segmentation
Over the past decade[5, 6], CNN-based U-Net families—including Res-UNet[8],
Dense-UNet[7], UNet++[45], Attention-UNet[10] and nnUNet[9]—have dom-
inated medical image segmentation due to their effective local feature ex-
traction and multi-scale representation capabilities.
However, since their
receptive field expands slowly, they inherently struggle to capture long-range
dependencies.
To compensate for this, Transformers, starting from ViT and evolving
into TransUNet[13], MedT[15], SwinUNet[48], and SwinUNETR[16], were in-
troduced to incorporate global self-attention and capture distant anatomical
structures. Yet, their quadratic computational complexity poses a significant
burden when applied to high-resolution or 3D medical data.
More recently, Mamba-style State Space Models (SSMs) have emerged
as a promising third paradigm.
SSMs[20] offer strong long-range depen-
dency modeling with linear complexity, making them well-suited for dense
prediction tasks. Notably, U-Mamba[23], a hybrid CNN-SSM architecture,
combines convolutions with Mamba blocks to achieve both local and global
modeling in a scalable encoder–decoder framework, outperforming traditional
CNNs and Transformers on multiple biomedical tasks like CT/MR organ,
endoscopy instrument, and microscopy cell segmentation. Building on this,
VM-UNet[36] introduces a pure SSM-based U-Net design by using Visual
SSM blocks[19] and an asymmetric encoder–decoder architecture. Thus, the
landscape of segmentation architectures has clearly evolved from local CNNs
to global Transformers, and now to scalable, long-range SSMs like Mamba.
4


Despite the progress, most existing works treat these paradigms in isola-
tion—either improving local context modeling via CNN variants or exploring
global reasoning through Transformer attention. SSM-based designs offer a
middle ground with favorable scalability, yet often lack high-level semantic
guidance, especially in semantically sparse medical images. Furthermore, few
works have investigated the fusion of state space modeling with external se-
mantic priors, such as those from large-scale foundation models. This leaves
open a promising direction for hybrid architectures that combine efficiency,
spatial coherence, and semantic richness.
2.2. Foundation Models in Medical Segmentation
In recent years, large-scale foundation models have begun to transform
computer vision, with the Segment Anything Model (SAM)[24] emerging as a
seminal example of prompt-based segmentation across diverse natural image
datasets. However, when directly applied to medical imaging, SAM’s per-
formance diminishes due to semantic and visual domain shifts that lead to
inaccurate delineation of pathological regions. To bridge this gap, researchers
have proposed several SAM-derived variants optimized for medical scenar-
ios. Notably, MedSAM[41], trained on over 1.5 million medical image–mask
pairs spanning 10 modalities and more than 30 cancer types, demonstrated
enhanced generalization across both internal and external datasets.However,
these adaptations largely rely on prompt engineering or data re-training, and
often do not address the need for structural consistency or efficient context
modeling. While MedSAM introduces extensive medical priors via super-
vised pre-training, it remains prompt-dependent during inference and lacks
explicit mechanisms for spatial alignment across slices or volumetric inputs.
On the other hand, BioSAM-2[29], based on SAM-2[30] and enhanced
with memory-augmented mechanisms, addresses both single-frame 2D and
multi-frame 3D biomedical segmentation tasks. Other techniques—including
modalities augmentation in MA-SAM[31], which injects 3D adapter modules
into SAM’s transformer blocks, and lightweight variants like MCP-MedSAM[17],
introducing modality and content prompts to reduce GPU requirements—have
further improved applicability across imaging modalities.
Many of these
approaches focus on input-side adaptations—either via modality prompts,
adapter injection, or multi-view learning—yet they seldom reconsider the
downstream decoder design. In particular, the fusion mechanisms and struc-
tural modeling strategies are often left unchanged, limiting the network’s
ability to align semantic features with anatomical details.
Additionally,
5


few methods incorporate mechanisms for capturing long-range dependencies
across spatially distant regions within a single image, which is critical for
segmenting dispersed or context-sensitive structures.
Despite these adaptations enhancing prompt automation, volumetric per-
ception, and modality-specific tuning, most remain focused on input form or
prompt engineering and have not integrated architectures designed for effi-
cient long-range context modeling, such as Mamba. In contrast, our approach
deliberately fills this gap. We first apply both LoRA[18] and Adapter[31]
modules to fine-tune SAM[24], instilling it with medical domain knowledge,
and then fuse its powerful visual representations with a Mamba-based en-
coder–decoder backbone.
3. Methodology
3.1. Overview
Existing vision Mamba models excel in long-range dependency modeling
for general images but struggle with global contextual perception in medi-
cal imaging. To address this, we propose Segment Anything Model-Based
Context Guided Mamba UNet (SAMBA-UNet), which leverages the Seg-
ment Anything Model (SAM)-a universal segmentation model with strong
generalization and deep global context extraction via its encoder. First, we
introduceCollaborative SAM Encoder Fine-tuning (CoSE), utilizing LoRA
and Adapter to adapt the SAM encoder to medical imaging. Second, we
integrate multi-level SAM encoder features as prior knowledge to enhance
Mamba features during decoding. Furthermore, to fully exploit SAM’s rich
contextual information and effectively guide Mamba feature learning, we pro-
pose a Hierarchical Context Enhancement Module (HCEM), as shown in
Figure 1.

3.3. Collaborative Fine-Tuning of the SAM Encoder (CoSE)
To adapt the Segment Anything Model (SAM) encoder to domain-specific
medical imaging tasks while preserving its pre-trained knowledge, we propose
a collaborative fine-tuning of the SAM Encoder (CoSE) that combines Low-
Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and Adapter modules.
This approach ensures
efficient adaptation while preserving the pre-trained knowledge of SAM’s
transformer-based encoder. Based on MedSAM [41], we incorporate LoRA
and Adapter into the Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and Feed-Forward
Network (FFN) of each ViT block, respectively. First, we fine-tune the Query
Q and Value V matrices of MHSA using LoRA. Specifically, as shown in the

and before the residual connection, while the second is positioned within
the residual path of the MLP layer. This integration effectively adapts the
pre-trained ViT block to medical image segmentation tasks.
3.4. Hierarchical Context Enhancement Module (HCEM)
To effectively integrate the global contextual knowledge from the Seg-
ment Anything Model (SAM) and the long-range dependency modeling abil-
ity of Mamba, we propose the Hierarchical Context Enhancement Module
(HCEM). This module enables deep fusion of hierarchical SAM features Sai
(i = 6, 9, 12) and Mamba skip connection features Mi (i = 1, 2, 3), ensuring
robust feature alignment and enhancement. Given the difference in spatial
resolutions between SAM hierarchical features and Mamba skip connection
features, we first perform a multi-scale transformation on Sai to unify its scale
with Mi. This ensures that features from both sources are aligned spatially
before fusion. The transformed SAM features and Mamba skip connection
features are then concatenated along the channel dimension:
Fcat = Concat(MS(Sai), Mi)
(3)
where MS(·) denotes the multi-scale transformation applied to Sai, and
Fcat represents the concatenated feature map. A 1 × 1 convolution is then
applied to adjust the channel dimensions, facilitating smooth integration
of both feature representations.
To further refine the fused features, we
introduce an attention mechanism based on both average pooling and max
pooling operations. The pooled features are passed through an activation
function to generate attention weights:
8


Fatt = σ(AvgPool(Fcat) + MaxPool(Fcat))
(4)
where σ(·) is the activation function, ensuring non-linearity in the atten-
tion generation process. The attention-enhanced features are then applied to
the original fused features via residual connections, ensuring effective feature
adaptation. Finally, the refined fusion features are concatenated again with
the original Mamba skip connection features Mi, followed by a 1×1 convolu-
tion to unify the output channels, ensuring compatibility with the subsequent
decoding process. This hierarchical fusion strategy leverages SAM’s global
contextual information to effectively guide the Mamba feature representation,
enhancing spatial perception and structural consistency in the segmentation
task.
3.5. End-to-End Training
We employ Binary Cross-Entropy and Dice loss (BceDice loss) for binary
segmentation, and Cross-Entropy and Dice loss (CeDice loss) for multi-class
segmentation, following VMUNet [36]. The loss functions are defined as:
LBceDice = λ1LBce + λ2LDice

4.3. Comparison with State-of-the-art Methods
To further validate the effectiveness and generalization ability of our
SAMBA-UNet framework, we comprehensively compare it against a wide
range of state-of-the-art (SOTA) medical image segmentation methods, in-
cluding CNN-based, Transformer-based, SSM-based, and SAM-derived foun-
dation models. The evaluation is conducted on three benchmark datasets:
ISIC17, ISIC18, and Synapse.
Comparison experiments on ISIC17 and ISIC18.The experimental
results on the ISIC17 and ISIC18 datasets, as shown in Table 1, demon-
strate the superior performance of our proposed method compared to exist-
ing approaches. On ISIC17, our method achieves the highest mIoU 81.07%
and DSC 89.54%, outperforming the previous best-performing VM-UNet by
0.84% and 0.51%, respectively.
Similarly, on ISIC18, our model achieves
the best mIoU 81.69% and DSC 89.92%, surpassing VM-UNet by 0.34%

We believe that traditional CNN-based architectures such as

fall short in modeling long-range contextual relationships. This often leads
to incomplete or blurred segmentation of diffuse lesion borders and results
in false positives—issues which hybrid Transformer-CNN models like Tran-
sUNet only partially address. Although TransUNet outperforms pure CNNs
(e.g., reaching around 89.8% Dice on ISIC-17), it still struggles with pre-
serving sharp lesion boundaries and involves heavier computation. Mean-

boundaries or demand heavy compute, and SAM-based methods falter with
prompt reliance or domain inconsistency. By contrast, SAMBA-UNet inte-
grates the global semantic awareness of foundation models with the efficient
contextual modeling of Mamba, enabling accurate, prompt-free segmentation
across diverse anatomical structures. Its ability to maintain high accuracy,
edge fidelity, and spatial coherence—without reliance on manual prompts or
post-processing—makes it particularly well-suited for deployment in auto-
mated clinical workflows.

5. Conclusion
We propose SAMBA-UNet, a hybrid segmentation framework that com-
bines SAM’s contextual knowledge with Mamba’s hierarchical feature model-
ing for medical image segmentation. By introducing the Hierarchical Context
Enhancement Module (HCEM) and applying LoRA and Adapter-based col-
laborative fine-tuning, our model effectively leverages global semantic priors
while maintaining domain-specific adaptability and structural precision.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that SAMBA-UNet achieves supe-
rior accuracy, boundary fidelity, and robustness across multiple datasets, all
without relying on prompt-based inputs. Its prompt-free and modular de-
sign makes it suitable for integration into real-world clinical segmentation
pipelines. In future work, we aim to explore mamba-based lightweight tun-
ing strategies to further optimize computational efficiency and extend our
framework to broader imaging modalities.
