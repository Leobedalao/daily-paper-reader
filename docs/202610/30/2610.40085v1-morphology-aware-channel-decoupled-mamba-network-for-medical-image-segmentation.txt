Abstract. Accurate multi-organ segmentation in computed tomogra-phy (CT) is essential for computer-aided diagnosis, treatment planning, and disease monitoring. However, substantial variations in organ shape and size, together with low tissue contrast and ambiguous boundaries, complicate the modeling of heterogeneous anatomical structures. To ad-dress these challenges, we propose a Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled Mamba Network (MCDNet), which integrates adaptive local structural enhancement with channel-decoupled long-range modeling. Specifically, we introduce a Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled State-Space (MCSS) block that divides feature channels into separate processing groups, al-lowing differentiated structural representations to be learned within an efficient state-space framework. Within each group, a Morphology-Aware Enhancement Module (MAEM) combines deformable convolutions with channel attention to adapt local feature extraction to anatomical shape variations. Group-wise state-space modeling then captures long-range spatial dependencies, and channel gating adaptively integrates the re-sulting features. Experiments on the Synapse abdominal CT dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of MCDNet, achieving an average Dice Similarity Coefficient of 81.61% for multi-organ segmentation. 

Keywords: Medical image segmentation · Morphology awareness · Chan-nel decoupling · State space models. 

1 Introduction 

Medical image segmentation plays a vital role in clinical workflows by enabling automated delineation of organs and pathological regions to support diagnosis, treatment planning, and disease monitoring. In abdominal computed tomogra-phy (CT), substantial variations in organ shape and size, low soft-tissue contrast, and similar intensity distributions between adjacent organs obscure anatomical boundaries and complicate structure identification. These challenges require seg-mentation methods to capture both the global spatial context of multiple organs and the local morphological details of individual structures. Existing approaches to medical image segmentation rely predominantly on deep learning. Convolutional neural networks with U-shaped encoder–decoder arXiv:2610.40085v1 [cs.CV] 29 Oct 2026 2 Wang et al. 

architectures, such as UNet[9], nnUNet[6], and UNet++[17], extract fine-grained features through multi-level skip connections and have become the backbone of many systems. More recently, Transformer-based models, such as Swin-Unet[3], TransUNet[4], and nnFormer[16], apply self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies and global context. However, the quadratic compu-tational complexity of self-attention with respect to the number of image to-kens imposes a significant processing burden. State space models[5] address this challenge by enabling efficient long-range modeling with linear complexity, mo-tivating a series of segmentation methods[13,18], including VM-UNet[10] and Swin-UMamba[8]. Despite these advances, efficient long-range modeling alone does not explic-itly account for the morphological diversity of abdominal organs. Structures with different shapes and spatial extents require different local representations, while subtle contours between adjacent organs are difficult to resolve using global con-text alone. Processing all feature channels through a shared state-space transfor-mation may also limit the capacity to represent these heterogeneous structural patterns separately. These observations motivate a joint design that adapts lo-cal feature extraction to organ morphology and decouples channel processing to support differentiated structural representations. To address these challenges, we introduce MCDNet, which integrates morphology-aware enhancement and channel-decoupled state-space modeling within a U-shaped encoder–decoder. Our Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled State-Space (MCSS) block divides feature channels into separate processing groups. In each group, a Morphology-Aware Enhancement Module (MAEM) adjusts convolu-tional sampling locations and reweights channel responses to capture local shape variations, followed by a State-Space 2D (SS2D) unit for long-range spatial mod-eling. An adaptive channel gate integrates the group outputs. This design con-nects local morphological adaptation with efficient global context modeling while allowing different channel groups to learn complementary structural features. • We propose MCDNet, which integrates morphology-aware local enhance-ment with channel-decoupled state-space modeling for medical image seg-mentation. 

• We design an MCSS block that processes channel groups separately and adaptively integrates their outputs, together with an MAEM that uses de-formable sampling and channel attention to capture diverse anatomical mor-phologies.

2 Method 

2.1 Overview 

As illustrated in Figure 1-(a), MCDNet first embeds the input CT slice into patches and processes the resulting features through a U-shaped encoder–decoder built from MCSS blocks. Each block combines morphology-aware local enhance-ment with channel-decoupled long-range modeling. A final class projection pro-duces a segmentation tensor of size H × W × C, where C denotes the number of anatomical classes. 

2.2 Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled State-Space Block 

The MCSS block couples adaptive local feature extraction with separate state-space processing of channel groups. As shown in Figure 1-(b), it incorporates an MAEM into each group before SS2D-based spatial modeling, allowing local shape information and long-range context to be modeled within the same pro-cessing stream. Given an input feature map X ∈ RH×W ×C , where C denotes the feature-channel dimension in this block, we first apply LayerNorm and a linear projection, and then split the channels into N groups. The group count is set to the number of segmentation classes, providing an anatomical basis for the channel decomposition. The resulting groups provide separate feature streams in which differentiated structural representations are learned end to end through segmentation supervision. 2.3 Morphology-Aware Enhancement Module 

To explicitly model local morphological variation, we introduce an MAEM within each channel group. Organs vary in shape, scale, and boundary geometry, mak-ing a fixed sampling pattern potentially insufficient for their local representa-tion. MAEM predicts deformable convolutional offsets from the input features to adapt sampling locations to local image structure. Depthwise–pointwise con-volutions then refine the sampled responses, and channel attention selectively weights the resulting features. 3 Experiments 

3.1 Dataset 

We evaluate our approach on the Synapse Multi-Organ Segmentation dataset[7], a publicly available collection of 30 abdominal CT cases comprising 3,779 axial CT slices. We report segmentation performance for eight anatomical structures: the aorta, gallbladder, left kidney, right kidney, liver, pancreas, spleen, and stom-ach. 

3.2 Evaluation Metrics 

We evaluate segmentation performance using the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) and the 95% Hausdorff Distance (HD95). DSC measures the overlap be-tween predicted and ground-truth segmentations, while HD95 assesses boundary discrepancy. Higher DSC and lower HD95 indicate better performance. 6 Wang et al.

3.3 Implementation Details 

We trained our model on a GeForce RTX 4090 GPU for 300 epochs. CT slices were resized to 224 × 224 , with a batch size of 22 and an initial learning rate of 

1 × 10 −3.

3.4 Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods 

We compare MCDNet with recent state-of-the-art medical image segmentation methods on the Synapse abdominal CT dataset. Quantitative results are re-ported in Table 1. MCDNet achieves the highest average DSC of 81.61% among the compared methods, improving upon VM-UNet and MedMamba by 0.53 and 0.94 percentage points, respectively. Compared with VM-UNet, MCDNet in-creases the DSC for the pancreas from 58.80% to 63.57% and for the spleen from 89.51% to 90.90%. These results demonstrate the performance of MCDNet across organs with diverse morphologies. Its design combines local morphological adaptation with channel-decoupled long-range modeling. MCDNet achieves an HD95 of 23.72, although several compared methods obtain lower HD95 values. 

4 Conclusion 

We propose MCDNet, a Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled Mamba Net-work for medical image segmentation. Its MCSS block processes channel groups separately to learn differentiated structural representations and captures long-range spatial context through SS2D. Within each group, MAEM uses deformable sampling and channel attention to adapt local feature extraction to anatomical shape variations. Adaptive channel gating integrates the group outputs, connect-ing morphology-aware enhancement with channel-decoupled state-space model-ing. Experiments on the Synapse abdominal CT dataset demonstrate improved average segmentation accuracy over the compared methods, achieving an average DSC of 81.61%.
